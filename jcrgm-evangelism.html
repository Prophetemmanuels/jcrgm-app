Evangelism & personal soul-winning

Clear gospel · genuine consent · loving follow-up

Evangelism is loving witness to Jesus in ordinary relationships and in organised outreach. We explain the good news faithfully, listen respectfully, invite a response and leave room for a person to decide freely. The goal is not to win an argument, collect a number or pressure someone into a public response.

📣

The gospel we share

  1. God: the Creator is holy, good and worthy of trust; people are made in God’s image.
  2. Our need: sin separates us from God and harms our relationship with God and neighbour; we cannot save ourselves.
  3. Jesus: the Son of God lived without sin, died for our sins and was raised bodily; he is Lord.
  4. Our response: by grace, turn to God and trust Jesus—not our merit—receiving forgiveness and new life.
  5. New life: follow Jesus in the Spirit, join his people, grow in holiness, love and service, and share the hope of the kingdom.

John 3:16–17 · Romans 3:21–26 · Ephesians 2:1–10 · 1 Corinthians 15:1–8

👂

A respectful conversation

  1. Pray and be present. Let the person be a person—not a target.
  2. Ask permission: “Would you be open to talking about what gives you hope?”
  3. Listen first. Ask what they believe, what they wonder about and what they would like prayer for.
  4. Share simply. Explain Jesus and your own testimony honestly; invite questions.
  5. Invite, never coerce. “Would you like to trust Jesus or learn more?” A no, pause or question is respected.
  6. Agree a next step. If they request contact, record only the details and permission they freely provide.

Colossians 4:5–6 · 1 Peter 3:15–16

🗣

A short personal testimony

Prepare a 2–3 minute story that points to Christ, not to the greatness of the storyteller.

  • Before: one honest sentence about a need, question or life direction—without glamorising sin.
  • Encounter: how you heard and responded to the good news about Jesus.
  • Since: what is changing, what is still difficult, and how Christ gives hope.
  • Invitation: ask what the person thinks or whether they want to hear more.
Be truthful. Do not invent miracles, guarantee a cure, promise wealth, exaggerate results or present a personal experience as a rule for everyone.
🧭

Prepare an outreach team

  • Pray for neighbours and learn the community’s real needs.
  • Set purpose, location, time, permissions, budget, safety plan and a named lead.
  • Train volunteers in gospel clarity, listening, safeguarding and respectful conduct.
  • Work in pairs or teams; use visible, public and accountable settings.
  • Offer practical help without making aid conditional on prayer, attendance or conversion.
  • Debrief, thank community partners and follow up only with permission.

JCRGM outreach training

Six-session evangelism course

Teach · discuss · practise

Run this as a six-week team or small-group training. Each meeting practises respectful witness, gospel clarity, safety and consent.

Session 01 · Facilitator guide

The good news: God’s rescue in Jesus

Read: Romans 3:21–26; 1 Corinthians 15:1–8; Ephesians 2:1–10

Teaching aim: Explain the gospel clearly without reducing it to a sales pitch or promising a quick fix.

Teach the text

  • God is Creator; human beings have dignity, yet sin breaks fellowship with God and neighbour.
  • Jesus lived faithfully, died for sin and was raised; the resurrection is central, not an optional ending.
  • Salvation is grace received through repentance and faith; new life begins a continuing walk with Jesus.

Full teaching guide · 15–20 minutes

Open and set context: Read Romans 3:21–26, 1 Corinthians 15:1–8 and Ephesians 2:1–10. Invite learners to hear the gospel as good news about God’s action in Jesus—not a sales technique or a promise that life will become easy.

Teach the main truth: Christian witness begins with God as Creator and human beings as persons of dignity, while also telling the truth about sin and its damage. Jesus’ faithful life, death for sin and resurrection stand at the centre. Salvation is grace, received through repentance and faith, not earned by good behaviour or a pressured prayer. New life means continuing to follow Jesus in community, learning love, holiness and service. Explain these truths in ordinary words, allow questions and do not promise wealth, healing or freedom from hardship as guaranteed outcomes.

Apply and pray: Invite pairs to practise a two-minute explanation using one passage and a question that welcomes dialogue. Ask what phrase needs simplifying for a neighbour. Pray for truthful, compassionate witness.

Discuss together

  1. Which parts of the gospel are easiest or hardest to explain in your setting?
  2. How can we speak about sin truthfully without shaming or stereotyping the listener?

Practice this week: Practise a two-minute gospel explanation using your own words and one Scripture passage.

Session 02 · Facilitator guide

Listen before you speak

Read: John 4:4–30; Colossians 4:5–6

Teaching aim: Hold respectful conversations shaped by listening, cultural humility and permission.

Teach the text

  • Ask open questions about hope, faith and what the person wants to discuss.
  • Reflect back what you heard before answering; do not assume a person’s beliefs or needs.
  • Offer prayer or a gospel conversation by asking permission, and accept “no” without punishment.

Full teaching guide · 15–20 minutes

Open and set context: Read John 4:4–30 and Colossians 4:5–6. Notice how a real conversation makes room for a person’s questions and context. Explain that respectful listening is not a trick to obtain agreement; it is a way to honour a neighbour.

Teach the main truth: Begin with permission and open questions: What matters to you? What do you believe about God? Would you like to hear how I understand this passage? Reflect back what you heard before responding. Do not assume someone’s religion, history or need from appearance or background. If a person declines, stop graciously. A short silence or an unanswered question can be more faithful than a rushed argument. Christians can speak with conviction while treating another person as an image-bearer, not a project.

Apply and pray: Role-play one conversation where the worker mostly listens and one where the listener says no. Ask what respectful acceptance sounded like. Pray for patience, courage and genuine love for the people JCRGM meets.

Discuss together

  1. What assumptions can Christians bring into a conversation?
  2. What does respectful disagreement sound like in practice?

Practice this week: Have one intentional conversation in which listening is the primary goal.

Session 03 · Facilitator guide

Tell your testimony with truth and care

Read: Mark 5:18–20; Acts 26:1–23

Teaching aim: Share a brief, truthful story that points to Christ rather than making the speaker the hero.

Teach the text

  • Describe life before without glamorising harm or sharing details that violate another person’s privacy.
  • Explain how you encountered Jesus and what you understood about the gospel.
  • Describe ongoing growth honestly; never invent miracles or claim every believer will have the same outcome.

Full teaching guide · 15–20 minutes

Open and set context: Read Mark 5:18–20 and Acts 26:1–23. Explain that a testimony is a truthful account of God’s work, not proof that every believer will have an identical experience or that the speaker is the hero.

Teach the main truth: Organise a brief story in three parts: what question or need shaped life before; how the person encountered the message of Jesus; and what is changing now, including ongoing struggles. Keep the focus on Christ and be honest about uncertainty. Do not invent miracles, exaggerate a result or disclose another person’s private story. A testimony can invite conversation, but it cannot replace a clear explanation of the gospel. Encourage speakers to choose details they genuinely consent to share and to consider their audience.

Apply and pray: Give learners two minutes to draft a testimony, then let a trusted partner check clarity, privacy and honesty. Pray for stories that point to Christ without pressure or exaggeration.

Discuss together

  1. Which details of a testimony belong to you to share, and which belong to other people?
  2. How can a testimony invite conversation rather than end it?

Practice this week: Prepare a 2–3 minute testimony and ask a trusted leader for feedback.

Session 04 · Facilitator guide

Invite a response without coercion

Read: Acts 2:36–41; Romans 10:8–17

Teaching aim: Offer a clear next step while protecting the person’s freedom and dignity.

Teach the text

  • Explain what trusting Jesus means; do not use fear, public pressure or emotional manipulation.
  • Allow questions, silence and time; prayer is an invitation, not a substitute for understanding.
  • Never promise wealth, healing, safety from hardship or social acceptance as a guaranteed reward for conversion.

Full teaching guide · 15–20 minutes

Open and set context: Read Acts 2:36–41 and Romans 10:8–17. Explain that the gospel invites a genuine response, but the listener’s freedom matters. A clear invitation is different from engineering an emotional moment.

Teach the main truth: Say plainly what trusting Jesus means and offer more than one next step: respond now, ask a question, speak privately later or take time to explore. Allow silence. Do not use fear, repeated pressure, public exposure or social status to produce a visible response. Prayer may support a person’s response, but it should not replace understanding or consent. If someone declines, thank them for the conversation and continue to treat them with dignity. Never report a response as a completed conversion if the person has not expressed it.

Apply and pray: Practise an invitation that includes an easy way to say no and a low-pressure option to learn more. Discuss how to respond if someone is distressed. Pray for clarity and gentleness.

Discuss together

  1. How can an altar call be made clear and voluntary?
  2. What should a worker do if a person declines or appears distressed?

Practice this week: Practise two invitations: one to respond now and one to continue exploring—both respectful.

Session 05 · Facilitator guide

Outreach as love of neighbour

Read: Luke 10:25–37; Matthew 5:13–16

Teaching aim: Plan public witness that is compassionate, safe and never makes aid conditional on conversion.

Teach the text

  • Listen to local people and partner with trusted community organisations before choosing a project.
  • Set the purpose, permissions, team lead, safety plan, budget and referral options.
  • Work in pairs or accountable teams; follow child-protection and event policies.

Full teaching guide · 15–20 minutes

Open and set context: Read Luke 10:25–37 and Matthew 5:13–16. Ask whose needs Jesus’ teaching brings into view and why compassionate action must not become a bargaining tool for conversion.

Teach the main truth: Plan service with the community, not merely for publicity. Listen to residents and trusted local organisations, identify what already exists and agree a realistic purpose, budget and safety plan. Offer help fairly; food, welfare, education or care must not be conditional on prayer, church attendance or a decision for Christ. Work in accountable teams, follow child-protection procedures and know referral options. Proclamation and practical love belong together, but no person should be forced to receive either.

Apply and pray: In small groups draft a one-page outreach idea with community input, safeguarding, consent and a way to learn from feedback. Ask what unintended harm to prevent. Pray for humble service.

Discuss together

  1. What need has the community itself identified?
  2. How could an outreach unintentionally pressure or harm someone?

Practice this week: Draft a one-page outreach plan for a JCRGM team and review it with leadership.

Session 06 · Facilitator guide

Consent-based follow-up and belonging

Read: Acts 2:41–47; 1 Thessalonians 2:7–12

Teaching aim: Move from an expressed response to patient discipleship, with permission and privacy.

Teach the text

  • Ask separately whether the person agrees to a limited confidential record and to follow-up contact.
  • Use the JCRGM response card only within its consent rules; if consent is absent, do not keep a person-level record.
  • Offer a next step—Bible conversation, service, group or baptism discussion—without assuming the person is ready.

Full teaching guide · 15–20 minutes

Open and set context: Read Acts 2:41–47 and 1 Thessalonians 2:7–12. Describe follow-up as patient companionship, not ownership of a person’s decision. The goal is to help someone explore belonging at a pace they choose.

Teach the main truth: Ask separately whether a person permits a limited confidential record and whether they want contact. Explain who will see the information and how it will be protected. If permission is absent or unclear, keep no person-level card and do not contact; use aggregate counts only. With consent, offer a Bible conversation, group, baptism discussion or pastoral contact without assuming readiness. A person may change their mind, and the church should honour that. For minors, follow guardian consent and safeguarding policy before any identifiable record or contact.

Apply and pray: Role-play a follow-up invitation and a respectful response when someone declines. Confirm who is authorised to handle records. Pray that every next step will protect dignity and support real discipleship.

Discuss together

  1. What is the difference between permission to keep a record and permission to contact?
  2. Who is authorised to receive a follow-up record, and when should it be deleted?

Practice this week: Role-play a respectful follow-up call, including how to respond if the person changes their mind.
