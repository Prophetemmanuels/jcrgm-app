🔥 Section 1

Vision, Mandate & Scriptural Foundation

The Welcome Ministry is not a door-keeping department. It is an altar where strangers become family, and a net where souls are caught for the Kingdom. Every other ministry in this house depends on what happens at our doors.

1.1 Our Vision Statement

“That every soul who walks through the gates of JCRGM — member, first-timer, backslider, mourner, dignitary or stranger — shall be received as Christ Himself, ministered to with dignity, and guided into belonging, discipleship and service.”

1.2 Our Mandate (4 assignments)

① Receive

To greet, welcome and seat every visitor in Jesus’ name — warmly, quickly and without embarrassment. No visitor is ever anonymous, ignored or made to feel like an outsider.

② Record

To capture the visitor’s details legitimately, respectfully and confidentially — so that the church can follow up, pray and care.

③ Relate

To connect every visitor to a real person — a host, a caller, a cell, a shepherd — within 48 hours of their visit.

④ Retain & Raise

To walk with the visitor until they are planted: rooted in Christ, joined to the family, deployed in service and growing as a disciple.

1.3 The Five Outcomes of Every First Visit

When a first-timer leaves JCRGM, they must be able to say five things:

  1. “I was expected.” Someone greeted me by the gate, called me by name at the desk, and saved me a seat.
  2. “I was safe.” I could park, I knew where my child was, the service was ordered, nothing embarrassing happened to me.
  3. “I was loved.” People were warm, not eye-ing me up. Nobody made me feel like a stranger or a project.
  4. “I understood.” I knew what was happening in the service, what the church believes, and how to find what I need.
  5. “I know what is next.” I got a call, an invitation, a class, a group — a clear next step and a person to contact.

1.5 The Seven Convictions of the Welcome Ministry

1. The visitor is sent

Nobody arrives by accident. Divine appointments have been arranged in heaven before they drove into our road — treat each one as sent by God.

2. The first impression is the sermon they read first

They may forget the text; they never forget the greeting, the seat, the smell, the order or the smile.

3. Nobody is a stranger in this house

There are no “outsiders” at JCRGM — only family who have not yet been introduced.

4. Dignity is not negotiable

Whether they come in a suit or in work clothes, in a car or on foot, we never embarrass, expose or shame anybody — not even “for the Lord.”

5. Follow-up is the difference

Churches do not lose visitors at the service; they lose them where there was no call, no visit, no contact, no next step.

6. Every servant represents the Lord and the Pastor

How you stand, speak, smell, dress and carry yourself is read as the character of this church and of Christ.

7. Welcome creates the room for growth

Souls are won by the message and kept by the atmosphere. Welcome ministry is the soil in which church growth takes root.

And one rule above all

Never let a visitor slip out unseen, unanswered and unfollowed.

2.2 Every Role, Described

🎯1. Welcome Ministry Coordinator

Suits: a mature, organised, warm believer with leadership experience (Acts 6:3 spirit).

  • Owns the whole ministry before God and the Pastor
  • Builds the rota and confirms every team member by Saturday
  • Leads the pre-service huddle and post-service debrief
  • Reports the 6 weekly numbers to leadership
  • Trains, corrects, encourages and restores team members
  • Manages the budget, gift bags, stocks and equipment
  • Represents the ministry in church management meetings
🪖2. Assistant / Sunday Service Captain

Suits: a decisive person who can think on their feet.

  • Runs the floor when the Coordinator is away or ministering
  • Assigns posts at the huddle and checks each post is manned
  • Solves problems in real time (seating, parking, disputes)
  • Keeps the time: no post left empty, no gap in the chain
  • Escalates anything serious to the Coordinator/Pastor immediately
🛡3. Gate & Parking Marshals

Suits: calm, firm, courteous; confident in public.

  • Welcome every arriving vehicle/person; direct parking
  • Help passengers alight, especially the elderly and mothers
  • Watch the perimeter; report anything suspicious
  • Escort first-timers from the gate to the reception desk
  • Umbrella escort in the rain; torch escort at night services
  • Control traffic at departure so nobody is stranded
🙋4. Greeters (Door Team)

Suits: smiling, warm, approachable, loud enough to be heard over the choir.

  • Stand at every entrance at least 40 minutes before service
  • Give the 8-second greeting to every single person
  • Identify first-timers early and walk them to the desk
  • Answer simple questions (toilets, seating, children, service times)
  • Never leave a door unattended until the sermon begins
💁5. Ushers

Suits: orderly, observant, courteous, discreet.

  • Seat worshippers, working front-to-back to fill space neatly
  • Give first-timers good seats (visibility, aisle, comfort)
  • Receive offering; keep order; watch for anyone in distress
  • Take the count of attendance and first-timers
  • Stand at the back during ministry; be ready for any emergency
  • Guide the altar call and counselling queue
📋6. Reception & Registration Desk

Suits: friendly, literate, careful with records, neat handwriting.

  • Welcome visitors with a name, a smile, a seat, a form, a pen
  • Help them fill the Visitor Card/QR form without pressure
  • Explain the church and the next steps on the pathway card
  • Issue the welcome gift pack
  • Record numbers, hand over the day’s cards to the Data Officer
🧸7. Children’s Check-in Team

Suits: patient, caring, trustworthy (safeguarding screened).

  • Register every child: name, age, parent, phone, class, pick-up tag
  • Never release a child to anyone without the matching tag
  • Comfort crying children; call a parent discreetly if needed
  • Explain your programme to parents on their first visit
  • Report any injury, incident or concern immediately, in writing
📞8. Follow-Up & Assimilation Team

Suits: persistent, prayerful, good on the phone, gentle.

  • Call every visitor within 48 hours using the script
  • Send the WhatsApp/SMS greeting the same day
  • Arrange the 7-day home visit or cell invitation
  • Log every contact in the follow-up register
  • Hand over converts to the New Believers’ Class
🚐9. Transport & Mobility Team

Suits: licensed, patient, punctual, safety-conscious.

  • Run the approved church transport route and pickup points
  • Provide wheelchairs, walking support, and seating for the elderly
  • Two-person rule: never transport a visitor alone
  • Log the trip: who, where, time, driver, marshal
  • Ensure nobody is left stranded after service — ever
🍵10. Hospitality & Refreshments Team

Suits: servants with a generous spirit (Martha with Mary’s heart).

  • Water, tea, biscuits for visitors and the elderly
  • Mothers’ room supplies and privacy
  • Visitors’ luncheon and new-members’ dinners
  • Kitchen hygiene, serving, washing up, storing
  • Budget requests for refreshments to the Coordinator
ℹ️11. Information & Media Desk

Suits: informed about everything happening in the house.

  • Keep the notice board, signposts and flyers current
  • Give accurate information on times, ministries, departments
  • Photograph welcome moments only with consent
  • Never post photos of counselling queues or identifying the vulnerable
💻12. Online Welcome Team

Suits: fast typists, kind tone, familiar with the platforms.

  • Greet every new viewer by name in the comments/chat
  • Share the QR visitor form and church contact in chat
  • Capture names of online first-timers for follow-up
  • Answer questions, never argue with critics
🔥13. Prayer Support Team

Suits: intercessors who love the secret place.

  • Pray over the gate, seats, desks and equipment before service
  • Pray through the visitor cards during the week
  • Pray for every team member by name
  • Keep the welcome desk under prayer during the service
🗂14. Data & Reporting Officer

Suits: meticulous, discreet, computer-literate.

  • Collects all cards the same day and enters them into the register
  • Keeps data confidential in a locked file / password-protected device
  • Prepares the weekly and monthly numbers for leadership
  • Never releases names, numbers or addresses to unofficial persons
🌸15. New Believers’ / Converts Team

Suits: grounded believers, able to teach simply.

  • Receive everyone who responds to the altar call
  • Take their details and pray with them
  • Enrol them into the Foundation/New Believers’ Class
  • Link each convert to a personal discipler within 7 days

2.4 Qualifications of a Welcome Worker

✅ Required

  • Born again, baptised and in good standing with the church
  • Regular attendance (not a Sunday-only visitor themselves)
  • Known character: honest, sober, patient (Titus 1:8; Acts 6:3)
  • Able to smile and speak to strangers comfortably
  • Punctual — arriving at T-90 without being chased
  • Willing to be trained, corrected and rota-ed
  • Free of scandal, drunkenness, violence, debt-chasing or gossip
  • Team player: no independent spirit, no self-promotion

⛔ Disqualifiers (until dealt with)

  • Current unrepented public sin or unresolved church discipline
  • Habitual lateness or absence without notice
  • Being under the influence, or smelling of alcohol, at any service
  • Being rude, harsh or mocking to people (especially the poor)
  • Using the post for business, loans, politics or romance-hunting
  • Stealing, including “borrowing” from offerings or storerooms
  • Spreading the visitors’ private information
  • Unwillingness to submit to the Coordinator and the Pastor

2.5 Recruitment — How We Fill the Team

  1. Announce the need from the pulpit with the character requirement, not just the vacancy.
  2. Invite personally. The Coordinator lists 20 names of warm, dependable members and invites them one by one — personal invitation beats announcements.
  3. Interview & reference — a short conversation with the Coordinator plus confirmation from their cell/house-fellowship leader.
  4. Orientation — hand them this portal, explain the covenant, agree the standards.
  5. Training — the 10-module course (Section 9), 6 weeks.
  6. Observation — serve 2 services beside a trained member, no post of their own.
  7. Supervised service — hold a real post under supervision for 4 services.
  8. Commissioning — badge, certificate, prayer of blessing and laying on of hands before the church.
  9. Placement — permanent post and rota confirmed in the Service Schedule Router.

2.8 The Pre-Service Huddle (T-60, 20 minutes)

Flow of the huddle

  1. Call to order & opening prayer (2 min) — pray over the doors, seats, desks.
  2. Roll call & post assignment (4 min) — every person receives a post; gaps filled at once.
  3. Roll call of last week (3 min) — numbers from last Sunday, names to remember, answers to prayer, visitors returning today.
  4. Word of the day (3 min) — one verse and one standard to emphasise today (e.g., “today our word is name — get three names”).
  5. Assignments (4 min) — the specific first-timers already known to be coming (invited by members); who receives whom; who escorts whom.
  6. Declarations & commissioning (2 min) — speak the team declaration (Section 13), then disperse to posts.
  7. Posture check (2 min) — badges on, phones silent, smiles up, glass of water for the throat, hair neat.

The 10-minute post-service debrief

Led by the Coordinator immediately after the last blessing, standing at the desk:

  • How many first-timers? Any returning second-timers?
  • Any first-timer who was missed or not registered?
  • Any problem: seating, noise, children, security, offence?
  • Any visitor needing urgent pastoral attention or prayer?
  • Any follow-up call that must happen today?
  • Names of members who brought visitors (for encouragement).
  • What will we do differently next Sunday?
  • Pray a short closing prayer and dismiss.

2.10 Conduct, Discipline & Restoration

Code of conduct — the 12 “never” list

  • Never argue with a visitor, or defend yourself loudly
  • Never discuss church politics, salaries or other members’ affairs
  • Never promise what the church has not promised (money, jobs, prophecy)
  • Never collect money from visitors “for prayer”, or outside the offering
  • Never touch a child or a person of the opposite sex inappropriately
  • Never give your personal number to a minor; use the church line
  • Never take a visitor’s details for personal use (including dating)
  • Never post about a visitor’s situation on social media
  • Never counsel an abuse survivor or a person in crisis alone or in private
  • Never sleep, sit and chat, or use the service as your own worship time while on post
  • Never criticise the preacher, the sermon or the church in front of visitors
  • Never leave the compound with a member of the opposite sex without a third party

Discipline ladder

  1. Private word — immediate, gentle correction (Matthew 18:15).
  2. Written warning — after a repeated issue, signed and filed.
  3. Suspension from post — 1 month, with retraining and restoration plan.
  4. Removal from ministry — for serious sin, referred to pastoral leadership.
  5. Restoration pathway — repentance, restitution, counsel, a season of testing, then re-commissioning when the leadership is satisfied.

Goal: restoration, never humiliation. Discipline is done privately, never discussed before the team or the congregation.

2.11 Caring for the Team (so the team can care for others)

  • Feed them. Arrange water and a meal/biscuits for workers after service — nobody serves on an empty stomach.
  • Pray for them by name at every meeting, and visit them when sick.
  • Recognise faithfulness publicly: “Welcome Worker of the Month”, certificates, a small token.
  • Cover their costs where possible: transport allowance for those who come far, airtime for the follow-up callers.
  • Rotate and rest them — no one serves two consecutive all-night services.
  • Grow them. Send your best ushers to Bible School and to leadership training — a growing team grows the church.
  • Protect them. When a visitor abuses a worker, the leadership defends the worker publicly and pastorally.
  • Never leave a worker alone to face a difficult person — the Captain steps in immediately.
The principle: a loved team loves the visitor; a drained team drains the visitor. Team care is visitor care.

The First-Time Visitor Journey — Everything That Must Happen

This is the master procedure. It covers the visitor’s whole walk with us: from the moment somebody invites them, through the gate, the service, the goodbye, and right through to 90 days when they become family. Nothing here is optional. Each stage names what to do, who does it, and what could go wrong.

The Golden Rule of the house: Never let a visitor be anonymous — not for one service, not for one moment, not for one exit.

Stage 1 — The Road, the Gate & the First 15 Seconds

  1. Signage & approach (T-30). Directional signs up: parking, entrance, toilets, children’s church, reception. Any first-timer must be able to find the door without asking.
  2. The driveway marshal greets first. Two marshals face outward at the gate — never chatting to each other. As the vehicle arrives: make eye contact, smile, raise your hand, direct to parking.
    MARSHAL: “Welcome to JCRGM! Good morning, sir/madam — you can park just here, I will guide you. (Then physically guide them.) Is this your first time with us?”
    IF YES: “Praise God — you are most welcome! Please allow me to walk you to the reception desk. My name is [Name].” (Never just point. Walk with them.)
  3. The walking escort. The marshal or greeter walks the visitors from the gate/car park to the reception desk — talking warmly, not rushing. On the way: “Where are you coming from? Did you come with family?” Collect the tone of the person: shy, excited, needy, hesitant.
  4. The door greeting (the 8-second rule). At the entrance door another greeter greets them again. Within 8 seconds of eye contact they must have: a smile, a welcome word, and the offer of help. This is the moment that decides whether or not they ever come back.
  5. Read their state. Is the visitor here out of pain (bereaved, sick, unemployed), or curiosity, or invitation? Adjust your tone accordingly — cheerful for the curious, gentle for the grieving.
What must never happen at the gate: nobody is eating, chatting in cliques, sitting on chairs, on the phone, wearing sunglasses indoors, chewing gum, greeting only the “smart” people, or turning their back on the door. This is a holy post — look at it that way.

Stage 2 — Reception Desk: Name, Card, Gift, Seat

  1. Receive them with honour. “You are welcome in the house of the Lord. Please have a seat here at the welcome desk — may I know your name?”
    DESK: “May I know your name?”
    DESK: “Mr/Mrs [Name], is this your first time at JCRGM?”
    DESK: “Wonderful. We would love to be a blessing to you. Please help us with these few details so we can serve you better — it takes two minutes. Would you prefer to write it on this card or shall I fill it in for you?”
    DESK: “Do you have children with you? How old are they? Let me take you to the children’s church first so you can worship in peace — we will take good care of them and give you a tag for pick-up.”
  2. Fill the Visitor Card or QR form (full field list in Section 6). Never force anyone. If they refuse, note a description so you can still greet them next time: “Lady in green dress, shoulder bag, sat near the middle.”
  3. No embarrassment rule. Never say “you’re not saved,” never comment on their dress, never ask if they are married, never ask about their job as though it were a status test. Ask only what you need to serve them.
  4. Give the Welcome Pack (Section 6.4) — bag, letter from the Pastor, service times, next-steps card, pen, tract, water, snack, children’s sticker.
  5. Explain the house quickly — the order of service, when the offering comes (and that visitors are not obliged), where the toilets are, the mothers’ room, and when the service ends.

Stage 4 — The 60-Second Rule: The Moment Service Ends

The final “Amen” is the most dangerous moment in church life: visitors can vanish in 60 seconds. Protocol:

  • Every visitor is assigned a person before service ends. The Captain writes on the desk sheet: “Visitor 1 → Grace; Visitor 2 → Kelvin.” Nobody leaves the desk until every carded visitor has a named person walking to them.
  • Go to them within 60 seconds. Reach the visitor before they reach their car — a handshake, a thank you, a name.
  • Invite them to the courtyard/refreshment point. Not “if you like” — a warm personal invitation: “Please come and take tea with us; the Pastor would love to greet you.”
  • Introduce them to the Pastor (or the Pastor’s wife / an elder) when possible — a 30-second honour greeting, not a long queue.
  • Farewell blessing. “Thank you for coming. Next Sunday we would love to see you again — may I confirm you on WhatsApp so I can send you the directions and the message notes?”
  • Nobody leaves on foot/stranded. Check transport for every visitor: who is dropping whom, who needs the church vehicle.

Stage 5 — Departure & Safe Handover

  • Escort them to the car / to the transport point / to the bus stop with a word of blessing.
  • If using the church vehicle: two people in the vehicle (driver + marshal); log the trip in the transport book.
  • Encourage but do not force: if a visitor wants to leave quickly, greet, thank and release them in peace, and follow up by phone.
  • Photographs only with permission, and only if they are smiling: never photograph anyone at the counselling desk, during prayer, or in tears.
  • Children released only to the person holding the matching tag.

  2. Same-day WhatsApp/SMS from the church welcome line (template 1, Section 7.4): “Thank you for worshipping with us today, [Name]…”

3.11 Special Cases — How to Welcome Different Visitors

👶 A parent with a crying baby

  • Walk them first to the mothers’ room (comfortable, screened, with sound) — do not let them feel banished
  • Provide water, tissue and a fan/seat
  • Offer to hold the child only if the parent asks
  • Send someone to fetch them for the closing blessing if they wish

🧒 A child who came alone

  • Register the child; ask for parent/guardian name and phone if any
  • Place with the children’s ministry; never leave alone in the sanctuary
  • Do not release the child unless a known adult with authority collects them
  • Report to the safeguarding desk; arrange a home follow-up with parents

♿ A visitor with a disability

  • Offer help; do not grab the wheelchair or the person without permission
  • Seat with clear access, near a fan/ventilation and the aisle
  • Speak to them directly, not only to the one accompanying them
  • Have a sign-language or interpreting plan where possible

🕯 A mourner or the recently bereaved

  • Lower your voice, slow your pace; do not interview them
  • Offer a seat, water, and silence; whisper “God is with you”
  • Refer immediately to the Bereavement/Counselling desk, never counsel them yourself
  • Follow up after the burial — not only before it

🎩 A dignitary, chief, government officer or guest minister

  • Receive at the gate, escort to reserved seating, offer protocol greeting
  • Do not swarm, photograph or interview them without clearance
  • If they speak to the press or ask about the church, refer to the Pastor
  • Offer water, programme and a thank-you note afterwards

💼 A visitor who came “for help” (money, job, food, fees)

  • Never send them away rudely; never promise money or a job
  • Listen, note the need, take their details, pray with them
  • Refer to the Welfare/Support Team (jcrgm-support-team.html) with a referral slip; the process decides, not the doorkeeper
  • Protect the church from scams: no gifts of money at the gate by individuals

🙇 A returning backslider

  • Receive with joy, no interrogation, no gossip about where they have been
  • Restore privately with pastoral care; assign a mature mentor
  • Let the past stay with Christ — make sure no usher or member reminds them of it

❓ A sincere sceptic or questioner

  • Welcome the questions; never argue or mock
  • Keep answers short; offer a booklet and a friendly meeting with the Pastor
  • Invite them to the Bible School / class that answers their question (origin, suffering, science & faith)

🧍 Someone who slipped out early

  • The empty seat rule: any usher who notices a visitor leaving early takes the seat number and reports it to the desk
  • Call the same day: “We missed you at the end — we hope you are well. Is there anything that troubled you?”
  • Never assume; sometimes it is transport or a child — the call recovers the relationship

📵 A visitor who refused to fill a card

  • Respect the refusal. Note appearance (dress colour, position, companions) so they are still recognised
  • Ensure they get the welcome pack and a seat host
  • Ask the member who brought them for the details instead — never chase the visitor

💻 An online-only visitor

  • Greet them by name in the livestream chat; send the QR visitor form
  • Repeat the welcome to “those joining us online” out loud in the service
  • Follow up exactly like a physical visitor: call, invite to a physical service or cell near them

🔁 A visitor transferring from another church

  • Honour their previous church; never criticise it
  • Ask for a transfer/reference letter where applicable and pass it to the Pastor
  • Walk them through membership rather than assuming they are already settled

3.12 Red Flags — Report to the Pastor the Same Day

These situations are never handled by a greeter or usher alone. Escort to the counselling desk / pastoral office immediately and report in writing the same day:
  • Disclosure of abuse (physical, sexual, emotional), or a child at risk
  • Talk of suicide, self-harm or hopelessness
  • A person seeking shelter, refuge or protection from a violent home
  • Someone medically unwell, fainting, seizing or in pain
  • Threats, aggression, intoxication, or a weapon suspected
  • Someone claiming to be a prophet/apostle and beginning to minister without authority
  • A person asking for or collecting money from others on the premises
  • Media, police, immigration or government officials arriving unannounced
  • A missing child, or a child unclaimed after service
The three-sentence rule for reporting: (1) What happened. (2) Who was present. (3) What I did / what needs to be done now. Written, signed, timed, handed to the Pastor.

🤝 Section 5

Approach, Greeting & Conversation — The Craft of Welcome

How we approach a visitor is a skill, and skills are trained. Learn the rules, the scripts, the body language, the cultural wisdom and the boundaries.

5.1 The Seven Rules of Approach

  1. The 8-Second Rule. Within 8 seconds of eye contact, the visitor must receive a smile, a spoken welcome and an offer of help. Silence at the door reads as rejection.
  2. The 4-Foot Rule. Don’t wait for people to come to you. Step forward to within comfortable conversation distance, but never crowd or block the doorway.
  3. Face Outward. On post, always face the arriving people, not each other. Teams that chat back-to-back lose visitors by the dozen.
  4. Name First. Get the name, use it, remember it. A visitor who hears their own name twice will come back.
  5. Never Point — Walk. Accompany the visitor to the place they need. Escorting is a form of honour.
  6. Serve the Whole Person. See the child, the walking stick, the wet shoes, the hungry child, the shy teenager — welcome the household, not just the adult.
  7. Hand Over, Don’t Hold On. A greeter’s job is to hand the visitor on to the desk, the host, the usher, the Pastor, the counselling desk. Never abandon them at the doorway, and never monopolise them either.

5.7 Remembering Names — Six Practical Techniques

  • Repeat it immediately: “Blessing — welcome, Blessing.”
  • Spell it aloud for unusual names: “Is that ‘Mweemba’ with two e’s?” — people love the effort.
  • Anchor it: link the name to a picture or feature (“Grace with the yellow head wrap”).
  • Write it down fast: the desk sheet. Do not trust memory alone.
  • Use it twice in the conversation and again at farewell.
  • Pass it on: tell the host and the MC the name — the whole team should be able to greet them by name.

5.8 Language & Culture (Serving a Zambian Family)

  • Use the common languages in the welcome. Learn these greetings by heart: “Mwapoleni” (Bemba greeting), “Monile” / “Mwe bwanji” (Nyanja/Chichewa), “Twalumba” (thank you — Bemba/Tonga), “Zikomo” (thank you — Nyanja/Chewa), “Mwabuka buti” (Bemba good morning).
  • Both hands to offer or receive anything from an elder; the traditional respectful gesture of kneeling/clapping of hands is honoured, never corrected or mocked.
  • Greet elders first, then women with children, then youth — respect order builds trust with the family.
  • Handshakes: offer the hand and, where the culture indicates, the wrist-touch greeting to elders. Where a woman prefers not to shake, simply bow with a smile and use your word — never force physical contact.
  • Do not demean anyone’s dress (chitenge, work uniform, school uniform, second-hand clothes). A person in work clothes may be coming from a night shift; treat them as the King’s guest.
  • Widows, single mothers and orphans — affirm, protect and honour; make sure nobody questions where their husband is.
  • Translator protocol: if the visitor speaks little English, call the duty translator at once; sit with them, translate the welcome, the offering instruction and the altar call. For a first-timer, translation is memory-making.
  • Tribe-blind policy: any comment about tribe, region or nationality is a serious offence in this ministry and is handled under discipline.

7.3 What to Do If the Phone Isn’t Answered

  1. Attempt 1: call between 10:00–12:00. If no answer, send a WhatsApp/SMS the same day.
  2. Attempt 2: next day, different time (evening 17:00–19:00).
  3. Attempt 3: day 4. If still no answer, and you know the area, arrange a visit or ask the person who invited them to help.
  4. After 3 attempts: mark “unreachable — member to assist”, never “failed”. Try again in a month, and on birthdays/festivals.

7.5 Home Visitation Protocol

Before the visit

  • Confirm consent by phone; agree day and time that suits them
  • Assign two people: same gender as the visitor where possible (a female team for a woman, especially if she lives alone)
  • Inform the Coordinator: who is going, where, when, expected return
  • Carry: a small gift (fruit, water, mealie meal/nuts), a Bible, the “Your First Steps” booklet, a contact card
  • Dress modestly and respectfully for the home setting
  • Pray together as a team before leaving the church

During & after

  • Greet the whole household, including children; remove shoes if that is their custom
  • Keep the visit 30–40 minutes unless they insist on more
  • One short Scripture, one testimony, one prayer — not a sermon
  • Do not interrogate, do not inspect their lifestyle, do not borrow anything
  • Do not accept gifts beyond a cup of water if it strains their household; do not eat a meal you can see they cannot afford
  • Answer questions; invite them to the cell and to Sunday; note any household needs
  • Never enter a home on false pretences or invite yourself in
  • Write and hand in the home-visit report the same day (form in Section 10)

  • Every visitor who lives within reach of a fellowship is introduced by phone first (leader to visitor), then met physically at a landmark the first time.
  • Cell leaders report attendance of new visitors back to the assimilation team within 24 hours.
  • If the visitor does not attend after 2 invitations, a team member visits the home with the cell leader.
  • Cells are the church’s retention engine: a visitor who joins a cell within 21 days is 5× more likely to still be with us in a year.

Winning a visitor is a service; keeping them is a system. Here is why people leave, how to make them stay, and how the Welcome Ministry fuels the growth of the whole church.

8.2 The Ten Retention Levers

1. Name

People stay where they are known by name. Names are read, learned and used — at the gate, the desk, the pulpit (with permission) and the call.

2. A first-week friend

Assign a peer of the same age and gender, not a leader. Friendship, not duty, keeps people.

3. A place to sit

A designated “welcome bench/row” with hosts, so no visitor ever sits alone in a sea of strangers.

4. A clear next step

One card, one date, one person. Confusion is the enemy of commitment.

5. Follow-up within 48 hours

The single strongest predictor of a second visit.

6. A small group

Cells turn a crowd into family. Target: in a cell within 21 days.

7. A task

Give them something to do — serving converts attendance into ownership. Ask, don’t wait for them to volunteer.

8. A pastoral touch

A Pastor who knows their name and situation. One personal conversation is worth ten announcements.

9. Celebration rituals

A monthly “New Members’ Welcome Sunday”: certificate, prayer, photograph, cake/tea. Belonging must be visible.

10. Continuous care

Sick visits, birthdays, weddings, funerals, baby dedications, exam prayers, bereavement support. Care beyond Sunday is what makes a church a home.

Invitation → Arrival → Welcome → Word → Decision → Follow-up → Fellowship → Discipleship → Deployment. Weak links at the door lose souls the pulpit already won.

The arithmetic

The farmer’s law

Sow (evangelism), water (follow-up), weed (pastoral care), reap (discipleship). No farmer sows and walks away — that is why follow-up is a ministry, not an errand.

The gatekeeper’s truth

Most people decide about a church within the first 8 minutes, before the preacher stands. The greeter preaches the first sermon, and everyone hears it.

The shepherd’s eye

Growth is not only numbers; it is the one sheep (Luke 15). Systems exist to make sure the one is not lost in the crowd.

The team’s reward

The souls you welcome become the workers who carry your ministry after you. Train replacements; leave an inheritance.

2 M2 — Understanding the Visitor: The Psychology of the First Visit

Duration 1.5 hours · Key verse 1 Corinthians 9:22 (“I am made all things to all men”) · Aim: the trainee can read and serve the different states and needs of visitors.

Teaching content

  • What the visitor is feeling: anxiety (“where do I sit?”), insecurity (“am I dressed right?”), guilt (“I have not been to church in years”), grief, hope, scepticism, curiosity.
  • The eight visitor types: the invited guest; the seeker; the wounded (bereaved, divorced, betrayed); the returning backslider; the transfer member; the curious neighbour/passer-by; the needy (help, job, healing); the hostile/complaining.
  • The 12 reasons visitors don’t return (Section 8.1) — internalise them; they are our checklist of failings.
  • Basic needs Abraham’s servant met: water for the feet, a place to rest, food, and honour (Genesis 24:32–33). Translate: water, seat, refreshment, dignity.
  • Listening skills: eye contact, no interrupting, reflecting back, silence, not pre-judging, no diagnosis.
  • What a visitor remembers: faces, names, tone, order, smell, cleanliness and whether someone cared.

Drill

Role-play 5 visitor types (the shy teen, the angry man, the weeping widow, the cheerful talker, the silent one). Trainees must read the state and respond appropriately in 90 seconds.

Assignment

Visit any public place (market, bus station) and greet 5 strangers warmly; journal what happened and how people responded.

Assessment question

Give four reasons a first-time visitor may not return, and the welcome-team response to each.

3 M3 — The Doorkeeper’s Anointing: Prayer, Presence & Discernment

Duration 2 hours · Key verse Psalm 84:10 · Aim: the trainee serves from a prayed-up spirit, not from personal strength.

Teaching content

  • The post as an altar: the Levites kept the doors of the house (1 Chronicles 9:23–27); Obed-edom and the ark; the gatekeepers of the temple.
  • Personal preparation: prayer, fasting (as able), Bible reading, confession of sin, forgiveness, guarding the tongue.
  • Praying the doors before service: “Lift up your heads, O ye gates” (Psalm 24:7–10); anointing the doorposts with prayer, not superstition.
  • Discernment: recognising grief, hidden pain, demonic oppression, pride, hunger for Christ, and danger — and what to do with each (mostly: pray, get the right person, stay gentle).
  • Spiritual warfare basics: the welcome post is frontline evangelism; pray defensive prayers; never operate in fear, arrogance or “spiritual showing off.”
  • The team altar: unity, no quarrels, no gossip (Matthew 5:23–24) — a divided team repels visitors.
  • Words: blessing vs. cursing; never “prophesy” at the door; speak life, keep confidences.

Drill

Team prayer walk: pairs pray over the gate, doors, desk and seats using three Scriptures. Each trainee prays aloud once.

Assignment

7-day prayer journal for the welcome ministry using the seven prayer points in Section 13.3.

Assessment question

How does the doorkeeper’s private prayer life affect the visitor’s experience? Support with Scripture.

4 M4 — Approach, Greeting & Communication Skills

Duration 2 hours · Key verse Proverbs 15:23 (“a word in season, how good it is”) · Aim: the trainee can greet, host and converse with any visitor confidently.

Teaching content

  • The seven rules, the WELCOME acronym and the 8-second rule (Section 5).
  • Body language, tone, distance, cultural greetings, both hands, respect gestures.
  • The 20 starter lines and the 15 things never to say.
  • Remembering names: six techniques, live practice.
  • Handling children, the elderly, the disabled, and men/women appropriately.
  • Active listening and follow-up questions; how to conversation-close gracefully.
  • Answering the four common visitor questions (“What time do you finish? What do you believe about…? Do I have to give? Where do I take my child?”).
  • Language: key phrases in Bemba, Nyanja, Tonga, Lozi; how to use the translator desk.

Drill

“Greeting assembly line” — trainees rotate through 6 stations (gate, car park, door, desk, crying child, angry man) and must complete each greeting in under 30 seconds with a smile.

Assignment

Record yourself greeting someone (or practise before a mirror and a friend); note three improvements.

Assessment question

List the WELCOME steps and explain why “never point — always walk” matters.

5 M5 — The Systems: Journey, Run-Sheet, Desk, Cards & Records

Duration 2 hours · Key verse 1 Chronicles 25:8 (“order”) · Aim: the trainee can run the desk, fill the card, and keep the records correctly.

Teaching content

  • The whole first-time journey (Section 3) — stages 0 to 15, walked through as a story.
  • The Sunday run-sheet with times and post duties (Section 4).
  • Desk set-up, staffing, tally-taking; the master stock list.
  • The Visitor Card: every field explained, and how to ask sensitive questions politely.
  • The QR/digital form and the tablet; what to do when the network is down (paper first, always).
  • The register book and Excel sheet; naming conventions; no abbreviations that lose people.
  • Confidentiality, consent, retention, and the “DO NOT CONTACT” flag.
  • Referral slips and how to hand a visitor to the right desk.
  • Gift-bag stock control and the welcome pack standard.

Drill

Simulated desk: 6 “visitors” with different situations (refuses card, no phone, has 4 children, speaks only Nyanja, is a dignitary, is in tears). Trainees must register/handle without offending, and correctly complete the tally sheet.

Assignment

Complete 5 practice cards accurately; time yourself — target under 3 minutes per card.

Assessment question

Which five fields on the visitor card are essential and why? What do you do if a visitor refuses to give their number?

6 M6 — Follow-Up & Assimilation

Duration 2 hours · Key verse John 21:15–17 (“Feed my lambs”) · Aim: the trainee can follow up a visitor biblically and practically from Sunday to 90 days.

Teaching content

  • Why follow-up is a command of the Great Commission, not a courtesy (Matthew 28:19–20; 2 Timothy 2:2).
  • The four windows: 6 hours, 48 hours, 7 days, 21–90 days.
  • The call script, tone, length, what to log, and what never to do on a call.
  • The home-visit protocol, safety rules and the two-person rule.
  • The cell bridge and how to introduce a visitor to a cell leader.
  • The Foundation Class, Membership Class, gift discovery and deployment.
  • The assimilation funnel benchmarks and the follow-up worker’s week.
  • Re-engagement of the slipped-away; handling silence and no-answers.
  • Prayer follow-up: closing the loop on every prayer request.

Drill

Phone drill: trainees call each other role-playing five outcomes — not answered, enthusiastic, complaining, needy, hostile. The class critiques the response. Then record a real practice call.

Assignment

Write the follow-up log for 3 simulated visitors using the form in Section 10; prepare a Friday reminder message.

Assessment question

Give the four follow-up windows with the exact action in each. What must you never promise or discuss on a follow-up call?

7 M7 — Difficult Situations, Safety, Safeguarding & Confidentiality

Duration 2 hours · Key verse James 1:19 (“swift to hear, slow to speak”) · Aim: the trainee responds correctly, safely and calmly to every hard case.

Teaching content

  • Walking through every scenario in Section 12 with the correct protocol (drunk person, mental-health crisis, begging, lost child, abuse disclosure, suicide talk, medical emergency, fire/electricity, theft/pickpocket, complaint, recording without permission).
  • Safeguarding: never be alone with a child; never take a child to the toilet alone; the two-adult rule; the open-door rule for counselling; no private meetings with minors.
  • Vulnerable adults: abuse disclosure protocol (listen, do not interrogate, do not promise secrecy, record verbatim, hand to the Pastor immediately).
  • Confidentiality: what may be shared, with whom, and when (safety overrides secrecy; duty to report).
  • Conflict de-escalation: lower the voice, step back, side-on posture, offer a private place, get the Captain, never argue.
  • Emergency basics: first aid, bleeding, burns, fainting, seizure, choking; the nearest clinic; the ambulance and police numbers; who holds the first-aid kit; the assembly point.
  • Security awareness: watch for unattended bags, aggressive behaviour, unauthorised filming, unregistered traders, and unauthorised ministers — but never profile people by dress, tribe or class.

Drill

Crisis simulation: 5 stations with instructors acting the roles. Trainees must respond within 30 seconds and report correctly in writing.

Assignment

Write the three-sentence incident report for two scenarios (a lost child; a disclosure of abuse).

Assessment question

A woman tells you her husband beats her. Write exactly what you say and do — and what you must not do.

8 M8 — Culture, Dignity & Sensitivity

Duration 1.5 hours · Key verse Romans 12:10 (“in honour preferring one another”) · Aim: the trainee serves every person with Christ-like dignity across culture, age, gender and ability.

Teaching content

  • Zambian hospitality traditions and how to use them in church (greeting elders, both hands, respect gestures, language).
  • Doctrine of the stranger: no tribalism, regionalism, classism, racism or xenophobia in the house of God.
  • Serving women well: dignity, safety, not touching without consent, honouring single mothers and widows.
  • Serving men well: respect, no shaming, confidentiality.
  • Serving children and youth: safety, tone, listening, no ridicule of their dress or phone.
  • Serving people with disabilities: mobility, vision, hearing, speech, epilepsy, albinism (protect from staring and stigma).
  • Serving the mentally/emotionally unwell and the addicted: compassion without foolishness; referral pathways.
  • Poverty sensitivity: never expose a person’s need publicly; never shame a child for a torn uniform.
  • Grief protocol: what to say and what not to say to the bereaved.

Drill

“Sensitivity panel”: trainees answer 10 awkward questions from the instructor (a widow with no money, a man in dirty work clothes, a Muslim visitor, a pregnant teenager, a person with albinism, a foreigner with no English).

Assignment

Sit with someone from a different tribe/age/background than yours this week and learn three things about them.

Assessment question

What do you say — and what do you never say — to a widow attending alone for the first time?

9 M9 — Serving with Excellency: Appearance, Time, Order & Attitude

Duration 1 hour · Key verse Colossians 3:23 (“whatsoever ye do, do it heartily”) · Aim: the trainee serves with the quality and discipline that honours God and attracts people.

Teaching content

  • Excellence as worship: Daniel’s spirit of excellence (Daniel 6:3); “not slothful in business, fervent in spirit” (Romans 12:11).
  • Appearance standard and personal hygiene; the badge; the uniform; the smile as part of the uniform.
  • Time: never late, never rushing, never leaving the post. What lateness communicates to God and to the visitor.
  • Order: one voice, one chain of command, one plan; no freelancing.
  • Attitude: no murmuring, no eye-rolling, no comparing posts, no “I am only an usher” mentality.
  • Teamwork: cover each other, no ranks-and-pride, no gossip; the weakest post is as holy as the pulpit.
  • Handling criticism from members and visitors with grace.
  • Personal witness: the welcome worker is an evangelist. How to share your testimony in 3 minutes; how to explain salvation in 5 minutes.

Drill

“3-minute testimony” — every trainee tells how they came to Christ, time-kept by the instructor.

Assignment

Serve one full service with perfect punctuality; get a written comment from the Captain on posture, punctuality and attitude.

Assessment question

Why is punctuality a spiritual matter for a welcome worker? What is the “one voice” rule?

10 M10 — Church Growth & the Welcome Worker

Duration 1.5 hours · Key verse Acts 2:47 (“the Lord added to the church daily”) · Aim: the trainee sees their post as the front line of church growth and takes personal responsibility for souls.

Teaching content

  • The growth funnel and the retention arithmetic (Section 8.7).
  • How the Welcome Ministry produces members, workers, and future leaders.
  • The metrics that matter and why we count (Section 11) — counting for care, not for pride.
  • The invitation culture: how every member can bring one soul a year; how to invite without fear.
  • Personal soul-winning: the 3-minute testimony, the “1-metre evangelism” principle, and a simple 5-step gospel presentation.

Invite one person to church this week (not a relative) and record the outcome honestly.

Handling common trainee problems

  • The chronically late trainee: private conversation, covenant reminder, then discipline ladder.
  • The talkative/narcissistic trainee: give them a specific task and time limits; correct the dominance privately.
  • The shy one: pair with a warm member; start them at the desk, not the gate; build them gradually.

  • The one who “knows everything”: give them responsibility to train others — under supervision.
  • The spiritually immature: slow down, mentor personally, do not give platform authority yet.
  • The wounded volunteer: listen, pray, rest them if necessary; never push a hurting person into frontline duty.

12.3 Escalation Chart

The Charge, Prayers & Declarations

Read the Charge at commissioning and annually. Pray the prayers at every huddle. Declare the declarations over the doors.

13.1 The Charge to the Welcome Ministry

Beloved servant of God, you are not appointed to a door; you are appointed to an altar. What you do in the next hour will be remembered longer than the sermon that is preached, because the first sermon a stranger hears is not spoken from the pulpit — it is spoken from your face, your posture and your voice.

You are standing where Abraham’s servant stood, where the Levites kept watch, and where Christ Himself stands and knocks. Every person who crosses that gate has been allowed by heaven to reach this house. Some are coming out of grief, some out of guilt, some out of years of wandering. Some are being sent to test the warmth of this family — and heaven is watching how you receive them.

Therefore: smile when it is hard. Serve when you are tired. Be fair when it is not convenient. Never let a visitor be anonymous. Never let a seat be empty while a stranger stands. Never let a child wander while the parent worships in fear. Never let a name be taken and left un-prayed for, unfollowed, unloved.

Guard the visitor’s dignity as you would guard the ark. Guard their secrets as you would guard your own life. Guard your tongue; a careless word at the door has sent souls back to the world. Guard your heart; bitterness will be felt by people you have not yet met.

You will be tired and unnoticed. Jesus sees you. You will sometimes be criticised by people who have never stood where you stand. The Pastor loves you; the church needs you; heaven keeps your record. Do not serve for the eye of man; the One who sees in secret will reward you openly.

And when the crowds come and the house grows, remember: you did not merely let people in. You welcomed the Lord Himself — for He said, “I was a stranger, and ye took me in.”

Now go, doorkeeper. Take your post. The Lord of the harvest is with you.
