Purpose of this document

This Constitution establishes the spiritual, governance, administrative, financial, membership, departmental, ceremonial, welfare, safeguarding and operational framework of Jesus Christ Redeems Global Ministries. It governs worship, holy ordinances, ceremonies and events, member care and support, leadership, discipline, property and all Church affairs.

LEGAL REVIEW: Before formal adoption or registration, have the final document reviewed by a qualified Zambian legal practitioner and aligned with JCRGM's registered particulars and applicable law (including marriage, employment, child protection and non-profit requirements).

PREAMBLE

PREAMBLE AND FOUNDATIONAL DECLARATION

Declaration

We, the members of Jesus Christ Redeems Global Ministries, acknowledging God Almighty as Creator and Sovereign Lord, confessing Jesus Christ as Lord and Saviour, and recognizing the authority of Holy Scripture, hereby establish this Constitution to regulate the spiritual, administrative, financial, ceremonial, welfare and operational affairs of the Church.

We commit ourselves to the proclamation of the Gospel, making disciples, strengthening families, developing Christian character, prayer, worship, evangelism, missions, holy living, service to humanity, care for the vulnerable and responsible stewardship of all that God entrusts to us.

“Upon this rock I will build my church; and the gates of hell shall not prevail against it.” — Matthew 16:18
“Let all things be done decently and in order.” — 1 Corinthians 14:40
CHAPTER 1

IDENTITY, NAME AND PURPOSE

The name of the Church shall be JESUS CHRIST REDEEMS GLOBAL MINISTRIES (JCRGM). The Church may establish and operate branches, assemblies, ministries, departments, missions, schools, institutions and projects under its name and authority, and may use the abbreviated name JCRGM in all official communication.

  • A Christian religious organization founded on the Lordship of Jesus Christ.
  • A non-profit ministry: no part of its income or property shall inure to the private benefit of any member or leader except as reasonable compensation for services rendered and approved in accordance with this Constitution.
  • Church resources shall serve approved ministry purposes only.
  • Members do not personally own Church assets by virtue of membership or office.
  • The Church shall operate according to its registration status and applicable law.

Vision: To raise a spiritually mature, Christ-centred and empowered people who fulfil God's purposes and positively influence families, communities, the nation and the world.

Mission: Preach the Gospel; make disciples; teach Scripture; develop prayerful believers; equip workers; strengthen families; reach children and youth; support vulnerable persons; plant churches; care for members in need; and participate in missions and community transformation.

Christ-centred faith, biblical truth, prayer, integrity, excellence, servanthood, accountability, love, unity, holiness, compassion, faith, order, hospitality and stewardship.

The head office of JCRGM shall be in Lusaka, Zambia, or at such other place as the governing bodies may lawfully determine. All branches, assemblies and ministries derive their authority from this Constitution and remain under the oversight of the central leadership unless separately and lawfully constituted with written approval.

  • The motto of the Church is “Empowered for Greater Works.”
  • The Church may adopt an official seal, logo, flag, letterhead and branded materials.
  • The official seal and symbols shall be used only on authorized Church documents and materials, and shall be kept in the custody of the Administration as directed by the Administrative Council.
CHAPTER 2

STATEMENT OF FAITH AND DOCTRINE

Articles 7–16 — What We Believe

  1. Holy Scripture: The Bible, comprising the Old and New Testaments, is the inspired Word of God and the final authority for faith, doctrine and conduct.
  2. Triune God: One God eternally existing in three persons — Father, Son and Holy Spirit — co-equal and co-eternal.
  3. Jesus Christ: The eternal Son of God, fully divine and fully human, conceived by the Holy Spirit and born of the virgin Mary; He lived sinlessly, was crucified for our sins, rose bodily from the dead, ascended into heaven and will return in glory.
  4. Salvation: Salvation is by God's grace through faith in Jesus Christ alone, expressed in genuine repentance, confession of Christ as Lord and a transformed life. Those who reject Christ face eternal separation from God.
  5. Holy Spirit: The Holy Spirit convicts of sin, regenerates, sanctifies, empowers, guides, comforts and distributes spiritual gifts for the building up of the Church.
  6. The Church: The universal body of Christ, expressed in local congregations called to worship, evangelize, disciple believers and serve humanity.
  7. Ordinances: Water baptism and Holy Communion shall be observed according to Scripture and JCRGM ministerial practice (see Chapter 7).
  8. Marriage and Family: Marriage is a sacred covenant before God between one man and one woman; families shall be nurtured in faith, love and godly order (see Chapter 7).
  9. Christian Conduct: Members shall pursue holiness, honesty, love, humility, purity, reconciliation and integrity, abstaining from conduct contrary to Scripture.
  10. Eternal Hope: Christ will return; there will be a resurrection of the just and the unjust, final judgment, eternal life for the righteous and eternal punishment for the wicked.
No teaching, ceremony, event or practice of JCRGM shall contradict this Statement of Faith. Doctrinal questions shall be resolved by the Presbytery under the Overseer.

CHAPTER 4

LEADERSHIP AND GOVERNANCE

  1. Overseer / Presiding Minister — overall spiritual and strategic oversight (Chapter 5).
  2. Deputy Overseer (where appointed) — assists and acts in the Overseer's absence.
  3. Presbytery / Council of Ministers — doctrine, ordinations, pastoral governance.
  4. Church Administrative Council — organizational, financial and operational governance (Chairperson, Secretary, Treasurer and members).
  5. Board of Trustees (where required by registration) — custodianship of property.
  6. Pastors, Elders, Deacons and Deaconesses — shepherding, service and church order.
  7. Department Heads and Branch Leaders — ministry execution.
  8. Ministry Workers — approved service functions.
  9. General Membership — the congregation, with voice through general meetings.
  • Pastors preach, teach, counsel, administer ordinances and shepherd souls.
  • Elders support pastoral oversight, prayer, visitation and spiritual counsel.
  • Deacons/Deaconesses serve in practical ministry: welfare, hospitality, service logistics and care.
  • Trustees hold and protect Church property in accordance with the law and this Constitution.
  • Council officers (Secretary, Treasurer) keep records and funds as defined in Chapters 11 and 15.
  • Leadership is servanthood, modelled on Christ (Mark 10:43–45).
  • Authority must be exercised responsibly, humbly and within constitutional limits.
  • Every leader remains accountable — spiritually, financially and administratively.
  • Church office shall never be used for unlawful personal enrichment, coercion or abuse.
  • Leaders shall maintain confidentiality and safeguarding standards at all times.

Leaders shall demonstrate Christian character, doctrinal soundness, spiritual maturity, faithfulness, integrity, competence, teamwork and willingness to submit to accountability, consistent with the biblical standards for overseers and servants (1 Timothy 3; Titus 1). Persons under unresolved discipline or of unbecoming conduct shall not hold office.

  • Leaders shall disclose any personal, family or business interest in a matter before the Church and recuse themselves from related decisions.
  • No leader shall award Church contracts, employment or benefits to themselves, relatives or businesses without full disclosure and prior written approval of the competent body.
  • Leaders shall not accept gifts or favours intended to influence Church decisions.
  • Breach of this Article is a disciplinary matter under Chapter 10.
  • Terms of office are as stated in appointment letters and policy; elective offices are normally three (3) years, renewable (see Chapter 14).
  • Office ends by expiry of term, resignation, retirement, incapacity, transfer or removal.
  • Removal from office requires fair hearing and follows the discipline process (Chapter 10) or a resolution of the appointing body for non-misconduct reasons, with written notice.
  • Any vacancy shall be filled promptly on an acting or substantive basis to avoid disruption.
CHAPTER 5

OFFICE OF THE OVERSEER

Role

The Overseer (Presiding Minister) is the spiritual father and vision-bearer of JCRGM, providing spiritual direction, doctrinal oversight, pastoral leadership and strategic direction for the whole ministry.

Responsibilities

  • Guard the spiritual vision, doctrine and values of the Church.
  • Preside over major spiritual, ministerial and administrative meetings.
  • Appoint or recommend senior leaders according to Church procedure.
  • Provide pastoral and spiritual oversight to ministers, branches and departments.
  • Represent the Church where authorized.
  • Approve major ministry initiatives, ordinations and church plants.
  • Promote unity, mentorship and leadership development.
  • Commission ministers and preside over ordinations.

Deputy and Vacancy

  • A Deputy Overseer may be appointed to assist and to act whenever the Overseer is absent or incapacitated.
  • Where the office becomes vacant, the Presbytery together with the Administrative Council shall steward the affairs of the Church and initiate the succession process under Chapter 14 without delay.

Accountability

Spiritual authority does not remove financial, legal, safeguarding or administrative accountability. The Overseer shall work with the appropriate governing bodies to ensure responsible governance, and is subject to the discipline and restoration provisions of this Constitution like every other member.

CHAPTER 7

All ordinances and ceremonies are sacred acts conducted under pastoral authority, in decency and order. No ceremony shall contradict Scripture, this Constitution or the Laws of Zambia.
  • Only authorized ministers may officiate at Church ceremonies; the officiating minister has final authority over the order of service.
  • All ceremonies must be booked through the Church Administration with the required notice and documentation (see Schedule G).
  • Ceremonies for members in good standing take priority in scheduling.
  • Every ceremony shall be entered in the appropriate register and certified where applicable (Article 38; Chapter 15).
  • Approved charges, where applicable, follow the tariff schedule approved by the Administrative Council (Article 39) — no minister shall demand personal fees.
  • Church venues used for ceremonies shall be respected; decoration, photography and outside vendors require prior approval.
  • Any civil registration required by law (e.g. marriages) must be completed — the Church does not conduct ceremonies that evade legal requirements.

  • Remarriage after divorce or bereavement is considered case-by-case under pastoral guidance, Scripture and this Constitution.
  • Cohabiting couples are counselled toward repentance and proper solemnization.

CHAPTER 9

MEMBER WELFARE, CARE & SUPPORT

“Therefore, as we have opportunity, let us do good to all people, especially to those who belong to the family of believers.” — Galatians 6:10
“Religion that God our Father accepts as pure and faultless is this: to look after orphans and widows in their distress.” — James 1:27
  • Caring for members is a biblical duty, not an optional charity: the Church shepherds souls, comforts the afflicted and relieves genuine need.
  • Priority is given to members in good standing and their households, then to regular worshippers and the surrounding community as resources allow.
  • All support is subject to available resources — assistance is an act of love, not a debt or entitlement.
  • Care is given without favouritism, tribalism, or discrimination, and with full respect for dignity and confidentiality.
  • Hospital and home visits: The pastoral team and visitation teams visit sick, hospitalized, bereaved and shut-in members promptly upon notification.
  • New members and absentees: New converts and members are followed up and integrated; long-absent members are contacted with love before any register action.
  • New births and milestones: The Church rejoices with families — visits, prayers and thanksgiving for newborns, graduations and recoveries.
  • Confidentiality: What is seen and heard during visitation stays within the pastoral team except where safeguarding or legal duties require disclosure.
  • Visitation records (date, persons visited, needs identified) are kept and reported to the pastoral office.

Purpose: To relieve genuine, verified need among members — including food, emergency shelter, urgent medical bills, funeral support, disaster relief and approved school support.

Sources: Welfare offerings and thanksgiving, an annual budget allocation, special appeals and earmarked donations.

How to apply:

  1. Complete the Benevolence Assistance Application (Schedule D), available from the Administration or Welfare Department.
  2. Attach supporting evidence (bills, quotations, medical reports, school invoices, funeral programme).
  3. The Welfare Committee verifies the need (including a home/ground visit where appropriate) and decides promptly — emergencies within 72 hours where possible.
  4. Where possible, payment is made directly to the provider (hospital, school, landlord, supplier) rather than in cash to the applicant.
  5. The applicant is informed of the decision in writing with dignity and clarity.

Rules:

  • Assistance is normally a non-repayable grant; the Church does not operate salary advances or business loans to members.
  • Caps per category and per household per year are set in the schedule approved by the Council and reviewed annually.
  • Committee members recuse themselves where the applicant is a relative or close associate.
  • All applications, decisions and payments are recorded confidentially and audited under Chapter 11.
  • Fraudulent claims are a disciplinary matter under Chapter 10 and forfeit future assistance.
  • Dissatisfied applicants may appeal once to the Administrative Council within 14 days.
  • Upon notification of death, the pastoral office and Welfare Department immediately: visit and comfort the family, help plan the funeral service (Article 34), activate practical teams (choir, ushering, transport, food/utensils, grave logistics where applicable).
  • Approved bereavement assistance (service costs, transport contribution, food, coffin support where policy provides) is released per the welfare schedule and available resources.
  • Condolence contributions from members are collected transparently, recorded and handed to the family with a full account.
  • Care continues after burial: follow-up visits, grief counselling, prayer and practical help for widows, widowers and orphans.
  • Annual remembrance and thanksgiving moments may be held for bereaved families.
  • Seriously ill members receive prayer, visits, and communion on request (Article 31).
  • Emergency need (accident, fire, displacement, sudden hospitalization) is fast-tracked: the Welfare Head and supervising Pastor may authorize immediate relief within set limits, reporting to the Committee within 7 days.
  • The Church maintains an emergency contact chain and, where possible, a first-aid and transport roster.
  • The Church may organize blood donation and health-screening drives with qualified professionals.
  • The Church maintains a register of widows, orphans, the elderly and vulnerable members, updated through visitation and department reports.
  • Registered persons receive regular visits, prayer, food and material support at set intervals (e.g. monthly/quarterly) as resources allow, plus priority consideration from the Benevolence Fund.
  • Orphans and vulnerable children are supported toward schooling, mentorship and safeguarding; the Church partners with lawful guardians and, where needed, social welfare authorities.
  • Empowerment (skills training, small-business support groups, literacy) is preferred over perpetual handouts wherever possible.
  • The Church provides biblical counselling: premarital, marital and family, grief and trauma, youth and life-skills, and repentance/restoration counselling.
  • Counselling is offered by trained, approved counsellors under pastoral supervision; complex medical, psychiatric or legal cases are referred to qualified professionals.
  • Counselling conversations are strictly confidential, except where there is risk of harm, abuse of a minor or vulnerable person, or a legal duty to disclose — in which case safeguarding procedures (Chapter 17) apply.
  • A private, safe counselling space and a booking system are maintained by the Administration.
  • As resources allow, the Church runs skills training, entrepreneurship support groups, education support and savings-group mentorship to lift members toward self-reliance.
  • Programmes are open and advertised fairly; selection is needs-based and minuted.
  • The Church may partner with government, NGOs and businesses for community development, subject to Council approval and doctrinal integrity.
  • Composition: Welfare Department Head (chair), a pastoral representative, a finance representative, and representatives of women, men, youth and branches where applicable. Members serve renewable 2-year terms.
  • Meets at least monthly, and urgently for emergencies; quorum is half the members plus one.
  • Duties: assess benevolence applications, coordinate visitation and bereavement teams, maintain the vulnerable-persons register, manage the benevolence budget and report monthly to the Administrative Council.
  • Reporting: anonymized statistics to the congregation; full confidential records to the Council and auditors.
CHAPTER 10

DISCIPLINE & RESTORATION

Purpose

Discipline seeks truth, protection of the flock, repentance, restoration, justice and Church order — never revenge, humiliation or oppression (Galatians 6:1; Matthew 18:15–17).

Grounds

  • Serious misconduct or immorality.
  • Fraud, theft or falsification of records.
  • Abuse, exploitation or harassment in any form.
  • Persistent disruptive, divisive or rebellious conduct.
  • Serious or persistent doctrinal violation and false teaching.
  • Misuse or misappropriation of Church funds or property.
  • Sexual misconduct.
  • Conduct seriously damaging to the Church's witness or unity.

Process

  1. Receive and document the concern in writing.
  2. Assess immediate safeguarding needs and protect vulnerable persons first.
  3. Conduct a fair, confidential inquiry by impartial persons.
  4. Give the person full opportunity to respond to the allegations.
  5. Determine a proportionate outcome (see ladder below).
  6. Document the outcome and communicate it in writing.
  7. Allow appeal within 14 days to the next higher body.
  8. Pursue restoration wherever genuine repentance is shown.

3Suspension from duties, office or public ministry for a defined periodPastoral Council / Presbytery

5Removal from membership (last resort, for unrepentant serious cause)Presbytery + Council, ratified as policy provides

Leaders Under Discipline

Leaders are held to a higher standard. Allegations against senior leaders are handled by an impartial panel including Presbytery members not party to the matter, with the same rights of response and appeal. A leader may be placed on precautionary leave during inquiry where the interests of the Church or vulnerable persons require it.

Restoration Pathway

Where there is genuine repentance, the Church provides a structured restoration plan: pastoral oversight, counselling, discipleship milestones and a defined review period, after which duties, office or membership may be progressively restored.

MANDATORY: Criminal conduct, abuse or immediate-danger allegations must never be concealed as internal Church discipline. Applicable legal and reporting obligations must be followed immediately (see Chapter 17).
CHAPTER 11

FINANCE, TITHES, OFFERINGS & ACCOUNTABILITY

Sources of Income

  • Tithes.
  • Offerings and thanksgiving.
  • Freewill contributions and donations.
  • Approved fundraising and events.
  • Welfare and benevolence offerings.
  • Grants and partnerships where appropriate.
  • Approved Church activities and facility hire.
  • Other lawful sources.

Budget

  • The Church operates on an annual budget prepared by the Finance Department, reviewed by the Administrative Council and approved before the start of the financial year.
  • Departments and branches submit annual plans and budgets for consolidation.
  • Material unbudgeted expenditure requires prior Council approval.

Tithes and Offerings

The Church teaches biblical stewardship and cheerful, voluntary giving. No person shall be coerced through threats, humiliation, manipulation or denial of ministry. Tithing records are confidential.

Benevolence and Project Funds

Earmarked funds (benevolence, building, missions) are accounted for separately and used strictly for their stated purpose. Diversion of earmarked funds is a serious disciplinary matter.

Remuneration

Allowances and salaries for ministers and staff are set by the Administrative Council (with Presbytery input for ministers), minuted confidentially, reviewed periodically and paid through official channels with statutory deductions where applicable.

Reporting

Quarterly financial reports go to the Council; an audited-style annual summary is presented to the congregation at the Annual General Meeting. Members may raise finance questions through proper channels.

CHAPTER 12

PROPERTY, ASSETS & PROJECTS

Ownership

All property acquired for JCRGM — land, buildings, vehicles, equipment, instruments, furniture, computers, intellectual property and investments — is held and administered for Church purposes only, in the Church's lawful name, and never for private benefit.

Asset Register and Custody

  • Maintain a permanent register of all material assets with descriptions, values, locations and custodians.
  • Title deeds, logbooks, certificates and agreements are kept securely by the Administration with copies filed separately.
  • Assets are labelled, inventoried annually and insured where appropriate.

Use and Maintenance

  • Church property is used responsibly; vehicles operate on a logbook system with authorized drivers only.
  • Planned maintenance schedules protect buildings and equipment; damage or loss is reported immediately.
  • Private use of Church property requires written approval and may attract a charge.

Procurement

  • Purchases follow approved budgets; above the Council's threshold, at least three written quotations are required.
  • Suppliers connected to leaders or workers must be disclosed under Article 27; the lowest compliant, best-value bid normally wins.
  • All procurement is documented and auditable.

Projects

  • Building and development projects require a written proposal, designs/approvals, budget, funding plan and an oversight committee.
  • Progress and financial reports are presented to the Council at agreed intervals until handover.

Acquisition and Disposal

Land, buildings, vehicles and other major assets may only be acquired, sold, transferred, donated, mortgaged or disposed of through a Council resolution (and Trustee/Presbytery concurrence where required) in compliance with applicable law.

CHAPTER 13

MEETINGS & DECISION MAKING

Types of Meetings

  • Annual General Meeting (AGM) — yearly; receives annual ministry, financial and audit reports; confirms plans and elective offices due.
  • Special General Meetings — convened for urgent or constitutional matters.
  • Administrative Council meetings — at least quarterly.
  • Presbytery / pastoral meetings — as scheduled.
  • Departmental and workers' meetings — at least monthly.
  • Branch meetings — per branch calendar.

Notice

  • AGM: at least 21 days' written notice with agenda and reports.
  • Special General Meeting: at least 7 days' notice stating the business (only stated business is transacted).
  • Council and committee meetings: notice per their terms of reference (normally 7 days).

Quorum

  • Council/committees: half the members plus one, including the chair or deputy, unless terms of reference state otherwise.
  • AGM / General meetings: one-third of registered voting members present. If quorum fails, the meeting adjourns for 30 minutes, after which those present constitute a valid quorum for ordinary (non-constitutional) business.

Voting

  • Decisions are by simple majority of voting members present, unless this Constitution requires a higher threshold (e.g. Chapters 18–19).
  • Voting is normally by show of hands; elections and sensitive matters by secret ballot.
  • The chair has a casting vote in case of a tie.
  • Proxy voting is not permitted unless expressly provided in approved regulations.

Minutes and Records

Material decisions are recorded with date, attendees, agenda, resolutions, vote counts and action points. Minutes are confirmed at the next meeting, signed, and filed by the Administration within 7 days.

CHAPTER 14

APPOINTMENTS, ELECTIONS & SUCCESSION

Ministry positions are filled by appointment, election, ordination or commissioning according to the nature of the office — spiritual offices by pastoral appointment with Presbytery concurrence; administrative offices by election or appointment as this Constitution and policy provide.

Selection Criteria

  • Godly character and good reputation.
  • Spiritual maturity and doctrinal soundness.
  • Competence and capacity for the role.
  • Faithfulness and track record of service.
  • Agreement with the Statement of Faith and this Constitution.
  • Ability to serve under authority and in teamwork.

Terms and Handover

  • Elective and departmental offices run for three (3) years, renewable on satisfactory service, unless the appointment letter states otherwise.
  • Outgoing officers hand over files, assets, credentials and reports within 14 days; handover is documented and signed.
  • Acting appointments cover gaps and do not exceed 6 months without substantive filling.

Succession Planning

  • Every key office (Overseer, pastors, council officers, heads) maintains an understudy or deputy being mentored for continuity.
  • Succession to the office of Overseer follows a written succession framework held by the Presbytery: prayerful identification, examination, Presbytery and Council concurrence, and formal commissioning before the Church.
  • Upon the resignation, retirement, incapacity or home-going of a leader, an orderly transition is implemented without power struggles — disputes follow Chapter 16.

CHAPTER 16

DISPUTE RESOLUTION

Believers must labour to settle differences within the household of faith (1 Corinthians 6:1–8). Every dispute follows these steps in order:

  1. Private discussion between the parties in a spirit of humility (Matthew 18:15).
  2. Mediation by a mature, neutral leader or elder.
  3. Referral to the Pastoral Council / Presbytery panel, which hears both sides and issues a written decision.
  4. Appeal to the Administrative Council (administrative matters) or the Overseer with Presbytery (spiritual matters) within 14 days.
  5. External resolution — professional mediation, arbitration or legal process — only where internal remedies are exhausted or the matter by nature requires it (e.g. criminal, contractual, employment).
  • Complaints are acknowledged within 7 days; resolution is pursued within 30 days where possible.
  • No member shall be punished merely for raising a genuine concern in good faith; retaliation against complainants and witnesses is itself a disciplinary offence.
  • Parties must not take internal disputes to social media or the press while resolution is pending.
CHAPTER 17

SAFEGUARDING, CHILD PROTECTION & SAFETY

Zero Tolerance

JCRGM maintains a zero-tolerance position toward abuse, exploitation, sexual misconduct, harassment, trafficking and deliberate harm against children, youth and vulnerable persons — in church, at events, in homes and online.

Safeguarding Officer and Team

  • The Council appoints a trained Safeguarding Officer (and deputy, preferably of the opposite sex) with a published reporting line.
  • The Officer maintains incident records, coordinates training and liaises with lawful authorities.

Safe Recruitment and Conduct

  • All workers with children, youth and vulnerable persons are screened (references, interview, background checks where available) and trained before deployment.
  • Two-adult rule: no worker is alone with a child in a closed or private setting; transport of minors requires parental consent and accountability.
  • Workers maintain godly boundaries: no secret communications, gifts, favours or physical discipline of children.
  • A signed Code of Conduct (Schedule F) is mandatory for all workers.

Safe Environments

  • Children's and youth venues are visible, supervised and age-appropriate, with proper registration and collection procedures.
  • First aid, emergency contacts and incident forms are available at all gatherings involving children.
  • Photography and video of minors follow Article 45 and require appropriate consent.

Reporting Procedure

  1. Anyone who suspects abuse or harm reports immediately to the Safeguarding Officer, a pastor or the Church emergency line — do not investigate alone.
  2. The Officer secures the safety of the person at risk first, documents facts, and notifies the Overseer/Council leadership.
  3. Where a crime or a child at risk is involved, the Church reports to the police / social welfare without delay and cooperates fully.
  4. The alleged offender may be suspended from duties pending outcome; confidentiality is protected; malicious false reports are disciplined.

Training and Review

Safeguarding training is conducted at least annually for all workers; this policy is reviewed yearly and after any serious incident.

IT IS A SERIOUS OFFENCE for any leader or member to conceal, discourage reporting of, or intimidate witnesses to abuse. Failure to report known abuse of a minor is itself a disciplinary matter and may breach the law.
CHAPTER 18

AMENDMENT OF THE CONSTITUTION

  1. Proposed amendments are submitted in writing to the Secretary, stating the clause, the proposed wording and the reasons.
  2. The Administrative Council (with Presbytery for doctrinal matters) reviews theological, administrative, financial and legal implications and obtains legal advice.
  3. The proposed amendment is circulated to voting members at least 30 days before the deciding meeting.
  4. Adoption requires a two-thirds (⅔) majority of voting members present at a quorate AGM or Special General Meeting.
  5. Where the law requires, statutory notification, filing or approval is completed before the amendment takes effect.
  6. Amendments are recorded with date and resolution number; members receive updated copies.

The identity of Jesus Christ as Head of the Church, the biblical foundation and the core mission of JCRGM are entrenched and shall not be altered in any manner contrary to the foundational faith.

CHAPTER 19

DISSOLUTION

  1. The Church may only be dissolved by a resolution passed by at least a three-quarters (¾) majority of voting members at a Special General Meeting convened with 30 days' notice, and through the lawful process applicable to its registration status.
  2. Upon such resolution, liquidators/trustees are appointed to settle all lawful debts and liabilities.
  3. After liabilities are settled, remaining assets are transferred to a lawful Christian or charitable organization with substantially consistent objectives, subject to applicable law.
  4. Assets shall never be distributed among members, leaders or office-bearers merely because of membership or office.
  5. Deregistration and final filings are completed and records archived as the law requires.
