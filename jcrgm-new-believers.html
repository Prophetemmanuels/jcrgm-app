New believers: a JCRGM growth pathway

Welcome & New Believers

Help each person move from a first response to rooted belonging and a clear next step.

From first visit to disciple-maker

A clear growth pathway for every Christian

Guide, not a spiritual ranking

People grow at different paces and may need to revisit foundations. A named, caring person should help each one take the next faithful step. Never measure maturity only by age, title, public gifts or attendance.

1
Visitor / seeker

Welcome & listen

Make a sincere welcome, explain what to expect, invite questions and offer a low-pressure next step.

  • Offer a host or contact person.
  • Ask permission before recording contact details.
  • Invite to a meal, service, Alpha or introductory Bible conversation.

Romans 15:7 · 1 Peter 3:15–16

2
First days / new believer

Establish in Christ

Celebrate their expressed faith, listen to their story, explain the gospel and arrange consent-based follow-up.

  • Connect with a trained, same-gender or appropriately supervised mentor where helpful.
  • Offer a Bible, a simple reading plan and prayer support.
  • Explain baptism, church community and next steps without rushing.

Luke 15:1–7 · Acts 2:41–47

3
First 1–3 months

Build core practices

Grow a steady rhythm of Scripture, prayer, worship, fellowship, repentance, generosity and obedience.

  • Join a new-believers class or small group.
  • Discuss assurance, identity in Christ, forgiveness and temptation.
  • Offer baptism preparation and pastoral conversation.

John 15:1–8 · Colossians 2:6–7

4
Growing disciple

Root, practise & serve

Deepen biblical understanding and Christian character; discover gifts and serve within a healthy team.

  • Study the whole Bible and core Christian doctrine.
  • Practise reconciliation, stewardship, spiritual discernment and care for others.
  • Use a role description, training and supervision for service.

Ephesians 4:11–16 · Galatians 5:22–25

5
Mature disciple

Lead with humility

Show tested character, reliability, teachability, healthy relationships and accountability before greater responsibility.

  • Receive mentoring, feedback and safeguarding/ethics training.
  • Learn to teach, shepherd, resolve conflict and make wise referrals.
  • Share leadership; do not build dependence on one leader.

1 Timothy 3:1–13 · 1 Peter 5:1–5

6
Disciple-maker / sender

Reproduce & send

Help others follow Jesus, form trustworthy teams, serve the wider community and support mission beyond the church walls.

  • Mentor another person and prepare a successor.
  • Join local outreach, mission partnerships or church planting.
  • Review the fruit, learn from mistakes and keep Christ central.

2 Timothy 2:1–2 · Acts 13:1–3

Starting Christians

New-believer foundations

Welcome · establish · belong

A new Christian needs friendship, sound teaching, patient answers and a clear way into the life of the church. Offer a pathway rather than assuming that a decision card or a single prayer completes the work of discipleship.

First week: a personal welcome

  • Ask what name and contact method they prefer; explain why contact details are requested.
  • Listen to their story and questions before assigning a course.
  • Offer a Gospel reading plan (Mark, Luke or John) and a simple prayer pattern.
  • Introduce a trusted church contact and show how to find services and groups.
  • Ask whether they would like a conversation about baptism, family questions or pastoral support.
  • Do not promise that every problem will disappear or that every prayer will be answered in the way expected.

Acts 8:26–40 · 1 Thessalonians 2:7–12

First month: teach essential practices

  • Who Jesus is; the cross and resurrection; grace, repentance, faith and assurance.
  • How to read the Bible in context, ask questions and apply it.
  • Prayer, worship, confession, forgiveness and learning to depend on the Holy Spirit.
  • Christian identity, the church as a family, healthy relationships and belonging.
  • Water baptism and the Lord’s Supper: meaning, preparation and church practice.
  • What to do with temptation, doubt, suffering, conflict and unanswered prayer.

Romans 8:1–17 · Ephesians 2:1–10 · Acts 2:38–47

First three months: root and connect

  • Complete an introductory course or one-to-one Bible journey.
  • Connect to a small group, Sunday worship and appropriate pastoral care.
  • Practise one manageable rhythm of Scripture, prayer and fellowship.
  • Discuss spiritual gifts, Christian service and stewardship without assigning a title prematurely.
  • Offer a baptism pathway, when appropriate, according to church teaching and the person’s own understanding.
  • Review how they are doing; offer professional or safeguarding referral where needed.

Colossians 2:6–7 · Hebrews 10:23–25

Foundations to teach over time

Scripture and the story of the Bible · the Trinity · Jesus Christ · creation and human dignity · sin and salvation · the Holy Spirit and spiritual gifts · prayer and worship · church and sacraments/ordinances · holiness and fruit of the Spirit · money and stewardship · relationships and family · work and calling · suffering and grief · spiritual warfare with wisdom · Christian hope and the return of Christ · evangelism and mission.

Use JCRGM’s approved statement of faith and teaching plan. Invite questions and distinguish essential doctrine from differences where Christians disagree.

JCRGM newcomer formation

Eight-session new-believer course

Teach · discuss · practise

A flexible first-steps course for personal mentoring, a new-believers class or a small group. Begin with the person’s questions and follow JCRGM’s teaching on baptism and church life.

Session 01 · Facilitator guide

Grace, repentance and assurance

Read: Ephesians 2:1–10; Romans 8:1–17

Teaching aim: Understand salvation as God’s gift and learn to ground assurance in Christ, not feelings or performance.

Teach the text

  • Explain repentance as turning toward God and faith as trust in Jesus.
  • Read assurance passages in context; distinguish conviction from condemnation.
  • Invite questions about doubt and avoid declaring the state of someone’s heart on the basis of one moment.

Discuss together

  1. What does grace change about how we relate to God?
  2. Where can a new believer seek help when doubt returns?

Practice this week: Read Romans 8:1–17 during the week and write one question for the next meeting.

Session 02 · Facilitator guide

Start reading the Bible with understanding

Read: Luke 24:13–35; 2 Timothy 3:14–17

Teaching aim: Practise a simple, repeatable way to read a passage in its context.

Teach the text

  • Read the paragraph before and after; identify speaker, audience, genre and main point.
  • Ask what the text reveals about God, people, Jesus and faithful response.
  • Use a trusted Bible translation and ask a mature teacher when a passage is difficult.

Discuss together

  1. What changes when we read the whole passage rather than one verse?
  2. How can Scripture correct our assumptions?

Practice this week: Read one chapter of Mark and note one observation, one question and one response.

Session 03 · Facilitator guide

Prayer, worship and dependence on God

Read: Matthew 6:5–13; Psalm 23

Teaching aim: Build a simple prayer rhythm rooted in Jesus’ teaching and honest relationship with God.

Teach the text

  • Prayer includes praise, surrender, daily needs, confession and forgiveness.
  • Silence and lament are biblical; prayer is not a formula to control God.
  • Join communal worship while keeping personal prayer honest and sustainable.

Discuss together

  1. Which part of the Lord’s Prayer is easiest or hardest to pray?
  2. How can the church support a new believer who has never prayed aloud?

Practice this week: Use the Lord’s Prayer as a pattern for ten minutes on three days.

Session 04 · Facilitator guide

The Holy Spirit and Christian character

Read: John 14:15–27; Galatians 5:16–25

Teaching aim: Understand the Spirit’s work in witness, holiness, comfort, gifts and the fruit of Christlike character.

Teach the text

  • The Spirit points to Jesus and forms love, patience, faithfulness and self-control.
  • Spiritual gifts are for building up others, not status or control.
  • Test teachings and impressions by Scripture, Christlike fruit and accountable church discernment.

Discuss together

  1. How do the gifts and the fruit of the Spirit serve the church differently?
  2. What is a safe way to raise a question about a spiritual impression?

Practice this week: Notice one opportunity to serve another person using a gift or skill.

Session 05 · Facilitator guide

Belonging to the church; baptism and communion

Read: Acts 2:41–47; 1 Corinthians 11:23–26

Teaching aim: See Christian growth as life in Christ’s people, with JCRGM teaching guiding baptism and communion.

Teach the text

  • The church is a worshipping, learning, serving community—not simply a building or audience.
  • Explain JCRGM’s teaching and process for baptism and the Lord’s Supper; make space for questions.
  • Belonging includes mutual care, accountability, welcome and service.

Discuss together

  1. What helps someone feel genuinely welcomed rather than processed?
  2. What questions should be answered before baptism?

Practice this week: Meet one ministry worker or group leader and ask about a next step in church life.

Session 06 · Facilitator guide

Holiness, forgiveness and healthy relationships

Read: Ephesians 4:17–32; Colossians 3:12–17

Teaching aim: Practise truthful, compassionate relationships and understand growth as a process.

Teach the text

  • New life involves putting away deception, bitterness and harmful speech.
  • Forgiveness does not erase consequences or require a person to return to unsafe contact.
  • Reconciliation is pursued wisely, with safety and accountability where harm has occurred.

Discuss together

  1. What is the difference between forgiveness and excusing harm?
  2. How can a believer repair a relationship while keeping safe boundaries?

Practice this week: Choose one small act of truth, apology or kindness—with safety and consent.

Session 07 · Facilitator guide

Stewardship, work and daily life

Read: 1 Peter 4:10–11; 2 Corinthians 9:6–8

Teaching aim: Connect discipleship with work, family, time, money, gifts and generosity without manipulation.

Teach the text

  • All gifts and resources are entrusted for faithful service.
  • Generosity is willing and proportionate; no leader may promise a blessing in exchange for money.
  • Vocation includes honest work, care for family, service and seeking justice in daily life.

Discuss together

  1. How can a church teach giving without pressure or shame?
  2. Which gift or skill could be used for a neighbour this month?

Practice this week: Make a simple personal plan for time, service or generosity and review it privately.

Session 08 · Facilitator guide

Witness, mission and next steps

Read: Matthew 28:18–20; Acts 1:6–8

Teaching aim: See every believer as a growing witness who needs community, mentoring and a next faithful step.

Teach the text

  • Jesus sends disciples to make disciples, teaching obedience and baptising in community.
  • Witness can be spoken, lived and shared through service; it must remain truthful and voluntary.
  • Choose a next step with a trusted JCRGM leader: group, Bible study, baptism conversation or service.

Discuss together

  1. Who has helped you grow, and who could you encourage?
  2. What support do you need for your next step?

Practice this week: Write a 30-day growth plan with one practice, one person to contact and one way to serve.
