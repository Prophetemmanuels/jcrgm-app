Ethics & Safeguarding

Protect the people who trust the church through clear conduct, safe practice, accountable reporting and wise referrals.

Trust is part of the witness

Ministerial ethics & conduct

A shared standard for leaders and workers

Church workers hold spiritual influence and often receive private information. That trust must be protected. Adopt a written code, explain how to raise a concern, and apply it consistently—including to senior leaders.

Ethical commitments

  1. Christlike character: integrity, humility, truthfulness, self-control, teachability and a life open to appropriate accountability.
  2. Respect dignity and consent: no bullying, intimidation, grooming, discrimination, coercion, humiliation or spiritual abuse.
  3. Maintain clear boundaries: appropriate physical, sexual, emotional, pastoral, financial and digital boundaries; extra protection where there is a power imbalance.
  4. Use spiritual authority to serve: never demand money, sex, labour, secrecy, allegiance or personal loyalty as a condition of prayer, prophecy, counselling or church belonging.
  5. Be truthful in teaching: represent Scripture and sources accurately; acknowledge uncertainty; correct public errors; do not fabricate testimonies, numbers, credentials or miracles.
  6. Handle prophetic ministry responsibly: submit words to Scripture and accountable discernment; do not use them to control high-stakes personal decisions or silence questions.
  7. Protect confidentiality: explain limits before care; share only what is necessary with authorised people; follow safeguarding duties and applicable law.
  8. Work within competence: pastoral support is not a substitute for clinical mental-health, medical, legal or specialist care. Refer when appropriate.
  9. Disclose conflicts of interest: gifts, purchases, hiring, family relationships, fundraising and compensation must be reviewed transparently by independent oversight.
  10. Protect church funds: never mix personal and church money; do not count, approve, reconcile and disburse funds alone; keep records and give honest reports.
  11. Welcome complaints: provide a confidential, accessible route; protect people from retaliation; use impartial review and fair process.
  12. Care for the worker: practise rest, supervision, peer support and ongoing learning; ask for help before exhaustion becomes harm.

Practical boundary standards

  • For sensitive conversations, meet in a visible, appropriate place; tell an accountable colleague the time and general purpose.
  • Do not start a romantic or sexual relationship with someone over whom you hold pastoral authority without stepping back and seeking independent guidance; never exploit a minor or vulnerable person.
  • Do not promise absolute secrecy. If someone discloses abuse or immediate risk, respond calmly, record only necessary facts, follow policy and report promptly to the designated safeguarding lead and appropriate authorities.
  • Keep counselling pastoral, time-bounded and supervised. Explain your role; refer complex or high-risk cases.
  • For prayer, healing or deliverance, explain what will happen, ask permission, accept “no,” keep appropriate witnesses/oversight and avoid unnecessary touch.
  • Never ask someone to stop prescribed medication or professional treatment. Encourage consultation with qualified health professionals.
  • Give financial advice and benevolence through an accountable team, not a private arrangement that creates dependency or conflicts of interest.
  • Use approved church communication channels where possible; do not privately message minors or share another person’s story, image, prayer need or testimony without informed permission.
  • Do not use a pulpit, prayer meeting or private revelation to retaliate against critics or pressure a person to remain in an unsafe situation.
Accountability that works: a written code needs a named independent complaint route, trained safeguarding lead, board oversight, anti-retaliation protection, record-keeping rules, a fair investigation process and a plan for immediate safety. A senior leader must not be the sole decision-maker in a complaint about themselves.

Safe church, safe people

Safeguarding, pastoral care & referral

Prevention · response · healing

Minimum safeguarding system

  • Written child and adult safeguarding policies, approved by leadership and accessible to workers and the congregation.
  • A trained safeguarding focal person and an alternative / independent reporting route.
  • Safer recruitment, references and any required screening before roles with children or vulnerable adults.
  • Codes of conduct, induction, ongoing training, supervision and signed role expectations.
  • Visible, accountable practice: appropriate supervision, suitable spaces, attendance/arrival/departure procedures, parent/guardian permission and safe transport plans.
  • Consent for photos, videos, testimonials, prayer requests, data collection and follow-up.
  • A written process for responding to allegations, concerns, disclosures, injuries and near misses.
  • Confidential, access-limited records; a retention and deletion schedule; no informal group-chat case files.
  • Regular risk reviews of buildings, events, online ministry, transport and overnight activities.

When someone discloses harm

  1. Listen calmly, take the person seriously and thank them for telling you.
  2. Check immediate safety and urgent medical needs. Do not confront the alleged offender or investigate yourself.
  3. Do not promise secrecy or ask leading questions. Do not blame, pressure or ask the person to repeat the story to multiple people.
  4. Write a factual, dated note using their words where possible; keep it secure and share only with authorised responders.
  5. Follow JCRGM safeguarding policy and promptly contact the designated safeguarding lead and the relevant competent authorities required by current Zambian law.
  6. Offer appropriate pastoral support and a safe referral. Protect the person from retaliation and avoid mediation that could put them at risk.

Psalm 82:3–4 · Matthew 18:5–6 · Romans 13:1–4

Urgent danger: contact local emergency / police / health services and the appropriate safeguarding authority. Internal church processes must not delay protection or a legally required report.

Pastoral care: know your role

  • Care with compassion, respect, consent, cultural humility and clear boundaries.
  • Ask what support the person wants. Avoid diagnosing, interrogating, making guarantees or taking control of their choices.
  • Use a team approach for complex care; keep appropriate supervision and brief factual notes where policy requires.
  • Build a referral directory for health care, counselling, social welfare, domestic-violence support, addiction care and legal assistance.
  • For suicidal thoughts, self-harm, psychosis, violence, abuse or immediate risk: stay with the person if safe, involve trained professionals and emergency services, and follow the crisis procedure.
  • For domestic or sexual violence, prioritise survivor safety; do not force reconciliation or couples mediation where abuse is present.

Privacy & consent in ministry records

  • Explain what is collected, why, who can access it, how long it is kept and how a person can ask questions or withdraw consent.
  • Collect the minimum information needed; restrict access; use secure storage; delete when no longer needed under approved policy.
  • Do not publish a new convert’s name, photo, testimony, phone number or prayer request without specific informed permission.
  • Obtain parent/guardian permission before processing a child’s personal details, in line with applicable Zambian requirements.
  • Do not store follow-up information in personal devices or share it to public WhatsApp groups.
Local legal note: Zambia’s Children’s Code Act and Data Protection Act are linked in the resource library. This page is not legal advice. Ask a qualified Zambian professional / relevant authority to confirm current reporting, consent, registration, storage and retention obligations for JCRGM.

JCRGM safety and integrity training

Six-session ethics & safeguarding course

Teach · discuss · practise

Train all workers, not only children’s leaders. Review JCRGM’s approved policy and local legal requirements before activities or case response.

Session 01 · Facilitator guide

Power, character and spiritual abuse

Read: Mark 10:42–45; Ezekiel 34:1–10

Teaching aim: Recognise how spiritual authority can protect or harm and commit to accountable service.

Teach the text

  • No leader may demand money, sex, secrecy, labour or allegiance as a condition of prayer or belonging.
  • Test prophetic teaching by Scripture and accountable community; do not use it to control high-stakes choices.
  • A complaint about a leader needs an independent route.

Full teaching guide · 15–20 minutes

Open and set context: Read Mark 10:42–45 and Ezekiel 34:1–10. Set the contrast between leadership that serves and shepherds who feed themselves. Explain that the lesson concerns patterns of power and the duty to protect people, not permission to label someone without a fair process.

Teach the main truth: Spiritual trust can be misused when a leader demands money, sex, secrecy, labour or unquestioning allegiance, or threatens a person’s access to God or community for saying no. A prophetic claim must not be used to control major decisions. Healthy ministry makes boundaries and decision rights clear, invites questions and has an independent route for complaints about senior leaders. Concerns should be heard without retaliation, handled confidentially as far as safety permits, and referred through the approved process.

Apply and pray: Ask learners to identify where JCRGM’s conduct code and alternative complaint route can be found. Do not invite personal disclosures in the class. Pray for leaders to serve humbly and for people at risk to be protected.

Discuss together

  1. What behaviours signal coercion or spiritual abuse?
  2. Who can hear a concern about a senior leader?

Practice this week: Review JCRGM’s conduct code and identify one missing safeguard.

Session 02 · Facilitator guide

Boundaries, confidentiality and pastoral care

Read: Proverbs 11:13; Galatians 6:1–5

Teaching aim: Set clear limits for care, relationships, messaging and confidentiality.

Teach the text

  • Explain confidentiality and its safety/legal limits before a pastoral conversation.
  • Meet in appropriate accountable settings; do not privately message minors.
  • Refer clinical, legal and specialist needs beyond a leader’s competence.

Full teaching guide · 15–20 minutes

Open and set context: Read Proverbs 11:13 and Galatians 6:1–5. Discuss the difference between keeping a confidence and promising absolute secrecy. Pastoral care is an act of love, but it must remain within the helper’s role and competence.

Teach the main truth: Before a conversation, explain who may need to be told if someone is in danger or a child may be harmed. Meet in appropriate, accountable settings; keep communication professional and avoid private messaging with minors. Do not diagnose, interrogate or take control of another person’s decisions. When needs are clinical, legal or otherwise specialist, a minister can support a referral without pretending to be the expert. Brief factual notes should be made only where policy requires, stored securely and shared only with authorised people.

Apply and pray: Practise a plain-language explanation of confidentiality limits. Ask participants to name one boundary that protects both the person seeking care and the worker. Close by praying for wise, compassionate listening.

Discuss together

  1. What should never be promised in a pastoral conversation?
  2. How can a leader protect both care and boundaries?

Practice this week: Practise explaining confidentiality limits in plain language.

Session 03 · Facilitator guide

Child and vulnerable-adult safeguarding

Read: Psalm 82:3–4; Matthew 18:5–6

Teaching aim: Prevent abuse through policy, training, safer recruitment, supervision and reporting.

Teach the text

  • Have written child/adult policies, trained focal persons and alternative reporting routes.
  • Plan supervision, transport, arrival/departure, media consent and online contact safely.
  • Use guardian permission and current Zambian requirements for children’s data and activities.

Full teaching guide · 15–20 minutes

Open and set context: Read Psalm 82:3–4 and Matthew 18:5–6. Focus on the responsibility to protect people with less power. Explain that safeguarding is prevention, safe practice and response—not merely a rulebook kept for emergencies.

Teach the main truth: JCRGM activities need written procedures, trained focal persons, safer recruitment and an alternative reporting route. Risk planning covers supervision, transport, arrival and departure, consent for images, online communication and appropriate adult-child boundaries. Workers should know what to do and whom to contact before an event begins. For children and adults at risk, use the current approved policy and confirm applicable Zambian requirements with competent professionals. Never promise that one safeguard makes an activity completely risk-free.

Apply and pray: Ask the class to choose an upcoming activity and identify two risks, a prevention step and the responsible adult. Do not use real case details. Pray for attentive care and the courage to report concerns.

Discuss together

  1. What is the difference between safeguarding and reacting after harm?
  2. Which JCRGM activities need a written risk review?

Practice this week: Complete a risk checklist for one children’s or outreach activity.

Session 04 · Facilitator guide

Responding to a disclosure or allegation

Read: Proverbs 31:8–9; Romans 13:1–4

Teaching aim: Respond calmly, preserve safety and report without conducting an informal investigation.

Teach the text

  • Listen, believe the person enough to act, and do not blame or ask leading questions.
  • Check immediate danger; do not confront the alleged person or promise secrecy.
  • Record facts securely and contact the safeguarding lead and competent authorities promptly.

Full teaching guide · 15–20 minutes

Open and set context: Read Proverbs 31:8–9 and Romans 13:1–4. Explain that protecting a person in danger includes taking a disclosure seriously and using the proper channels; it does not mean conducting an amateur investigation.

Teach the main truth: Listen calmly, thank the person for telling you and avoid blame or leading questions. Ask only what is needed to understand immediate safety. Do not confront the alleged person, promise secrecy or circulate the story among leaders. Record the person’s words and observable facts accurately, store the note securely and contact the designated safeguarding lead and competent authorities as required by current policy and law. If there is immediate danger, prioritise urgent safety and emergency support. Do not let internal reputation management delay protection.

Apply and pray: Practise the first response with a fictional scenario: listen, check immediate safety, record facts and report. Identify JCRGM’s primary and alternative contacts. Pray for courage and care for everyone affected.

Discuss together

  1. What is the first safe action if a child discloses harm?
  2. What should be excluded from a factual record?

Practice this week: Rehearse the JCRGM reporting pathway using a fictional scenario.

Session 05 · Facilitator guide

Consent, data protection and responsible records

Read: Proverbs 25:9–10; 1 Corinthians 13:4–7

Teaching aim: Collect the minimum information with permission and protect it throughout its life cycle.

Teach the text

  • Explain purpose, access, retention and contact choices in plain language.
  • Separate consent to record from consent to follow up; no permission means no person-level card.
  • Restrict access, avoid personal phones/public groups and delete under approved policy.

Full teaching guide · 15–20 minutes

Open and set context: Read Proverbs 25:9–10 and 1 Corinthians 13:4–7. Explain that privacy is a practical expression of love: personal information should not become gossip, publicity or an uncontrolled list.

Teach the main truth: Before collecting information, state the purpose, who can see it, how long it is needed and how the person can ask questions or withdraw permission. Consent to keep a record is separate from consent to make contact. Collect only what is necessary, restrict access, use approved secure storage and dispose of records under policy. Do not put sensitive details in public messaging groups or personal devices. For children’s information and data-controller duties, confirm the current Zambian requirements with a qualified local adviser; this teaching is not legal advice.

Apply and pray: Ask participants to audit a sample form for unnecessary fields and unclear consent. Identify who owns the retention schedule. Pray that JCRGM will handle every record with honesty and care.

Discuss together

  1. What details should never be on a soul-winning card?
  2. How does a person withdraw permission for follow-up?

Practice this week: Audit one paper or digital form for unnecessary personal data.

Session 06 · Facilitator guide

Pastoral care, crisis and worker wellbeing

Read: Romans 12:15; 2 Corinthians 1:3–7

Teaching aim: Offer humane support and timely referral without treating faith as a substitute for professional care.

Teach the text

  • Take distress seriously; do not diagnose, shame or promise outcomes.
  • For immediate risk, involve appropriate emergency and health professionals.
  • Prioritise survivor safety; do not force reconciliation or couples mediation where abuse is present.

Full teaching guide · 15–20 minutes

Open and set context: Read Romans 12:15 and 2 Corinthians 1:3–7. Teach that compassion includes presence, appropriate referral and care for the caregiver. Faith should never be used to shame someone for symptoms or to promise outcomes a minister cannot control.

Teach the main truth: For a crisis, follow the approved response plan, check immediate safety and involve trained health, welfare or emergency professionals. Do not diagnose, leave a person at imminent risk alone when safe support is available, or substitute prayer for urgent care. In abuse situations, prioritise survivor safety; do not force reconciliation or couples mediation. Ministers also need supervision, rest, peer support and clear workload limits. These protect the community because exhausted workers and secretive systems increase avoidable risk.

Apply and pray: Build a referral list with a named owner and review date, without sharing private cases. Ask what support workers need to continue safely. Close with an optional prayer for people in crisis and for those who care for them.

Discuss together

  1. What local referral contacts should JCRGM keep current?
  2. How can ministers seek supervision and rest?

Practice this week: Create a verified referral-list owner and a review date.
