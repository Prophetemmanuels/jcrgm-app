Leadership development & ministry workers

Calling · character · competence

A healthy leadership pathway

  1. Discover: listen for gifts, calling, character, availability and the person’s own sense of direction.
  2. Discern: assess fit with the church’s doctrine, values, safeguarding policy and actual ministry need.
  3. Prepare: give role description, code of conduct, policy orientation and practical training.
  4. Practise: serve with an experienced leader; start with supervised responsibility.
  5. Commission: clarify authority, accountability, resources and decision rights.
  6. Support: offer regular supervision, feedback, rest, learning and access to pastoral care.
  7. Review / release: review fruit and well-being; affirm, retrain, change roles or end a role fairly when appropriate.

Every ministry team should have

  • A one-sentence purpose aligned with the church mission.
  • A named lead and deputy; clear reporting line and decision limits.
  • Simple role descriptions, rota and volunteer code of conduct.
  • Required doctrine, safety, privacy and skills training before service.
  • Annual plan, approved budget, equipment list and risk check.
  • Accessible ways to raise concerns, give feedback or request support.
  • A succession plan so the ministry does not depend on one person.

Training every church worker needs

Spiritual formation

Gospel, Bible handling, prayer, worship, humility and personal discipleship.

People & boundaries

Listening, consent, confidentiality limits, safeguarding, inclusion and referral.

Role competence

Service skills, communication, conflict resolution, emergency basics and digital practice.

Accountability

Reporting lines, financial controls, complaints, incident records and supervision.

Session 01 · Facilitator guide

Calling, character and tested service

Read: 1 Timothy 3:1–13; Titus 1:5–9

Teaching aim: Discern leadership through character, calling, competence and community confirmation.

Teach the text

  • Gifting is not a substitute for tested character.
  • Leaders remain accountable and should be willing to learn and receive correction.
  • Appointment processes should be transparent and consistent.

Discuss together

  1. What evidence of character should precede responsibility?
  2. How can a leader ask for feedback safely?

Practice this week: Write a personal development goal and ask a mentor to review it.

Session 02 · Facilitator guide

Teach Scripture faithfully

Read: 2 Timothy 2:14–16; James 3:1

Teaching aim: Prepare teaching that follows the text and serves the church.

Teach the text

  • Study passage context, main idea, doctrinal connections and practical response.
  • Use sources honestly and distinguish biblical teaching from personal opinion.
  • Create space for questions; correct errors promptly.

Discuss together

  1. What checks help prevent a favourite idea from distorting a passage?
  2. How can a sermon become more accessible without becoming shallow?

Practice this week: Prepare a 10-minute Bible lesson using the JCRGM teaching template.

Session 03 · Facilitator guide

Servant authority and healthy power

Read: Mark 10:35–45; John 13:1–17

Teaching aim: Exercise authority to equip and protect people, not to demand personal loyalty.

Teach the text

  • Make decision rights clear; invite appropriate consultation.
  • Never use prophecy, pulpit, discipline or access to God to coerce.
  • Senior leaders require independent oversight and complaint pathways.

Discuss together

  1. What is the difference between spiritual guidance and control?
  2. How should a leader respond to disagreement?

Practice this week: Ask a team member what would make their role safer and clearer.

Session 04 · Facilitator guide

Pastoral care and referral

Read: 1 Thessalonians 2:7–12; Romans 12:15

Teaching aim: Care with compassion while recognising limits and referring specialist needs.

Teach the text

  • Listen before advising; explain confidentiality limits.
  • Avoid diagnosis and counselling beyond competence.
  • Use safeguarding and professional referrals for high-risk cases.

Discuss together

  1. What can a church offer that complements professional care?
  2. When should a leader seek supervision?

Practice this week: Create or review a local referral directory with leadership.

Session 05 · Facilitator guide

Build teams and handle conflict

Read: Ephesians 4:1–16; Philippians 2:1–11

Teaching aim: Form teams with clear roles, feedback, fair process and shared leadership.

Teach the text

  • Use role charters, training, rota, supervision and succession.
  • Address low-risk conflict directly and respectfully; use impartial process where needed.
  • Do not force mediation where abuse or coercion is alleged.

Discuss together

  1. How does role clarity prevent conflict?
  2. What support does a volunteer need before being asked to serve?

Practice this week: Run a team check-in with one appreciation, one concern and one next step.

Session 06 · Facilitator guide

Governance, finance and stewardship

Read: 2 Corinthians 8:16–21; 1 Peter 4:10–11

Teaching aim: Protect church resources through transparency, budgeting and shared controls.

Teach the text

  • Separate approval, custody, recording and reconciliation where possible.
  • Disclose conflicts of interest and follow approved procurement/benevolence policies.
  • Report accurately; church funds never become personal funds.

Discuss together

  1. Which controls protect both money and workers?
  2. How should a conflict of interest be recorded?

Practice this week: Walk through one JCRGM process with the responsible oversight team.

Session 07 · Facilitator guide

Ethics, safeguarding and complaints

Read: Micah 6:8; Matthew 18:5–6

Teaching aim: Lead a culture where concerns are heard and people are protected from retaliation.

Teach the text

  • Apply written conduct and safeguarding policies to every leader.
  • Use designated and independent reporting routes; keep factual records securely.
  • Report promptly to appropriate authorities when law or immediate safety requires.

Discuss together

  1. Why must a senior leader not investigate a complaint about themselves?
  2. How can the church reduce fear of retaliation?

Practice this week: Confirm that every worker knows the safeguarding focal person and alternative route.

Session 08 · Facilitator guide

Mission, succession and worker wellbeing

Read: Acts 13:1–3; 2 Timothy 2:1–2

Teaching aim: Send leaders well, prepare successors and sustain healthy service.

Teach the text

  • Mission requires prayer, training, budget, safety and accountable sending.
  • Succession is part of faithful leadership, not a threat to a leader’s value.
  • Rest, supervision and family care help prevent avoidable harm.

Discuss together

  1. What would be lost if one leader stepped away tomorrow?
  2. How can a team protect rest during busy seasons?

Practice this week: Identify one successor to coach and one workload to simplify.
