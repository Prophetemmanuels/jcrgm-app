Discipleship & Christian Maturity

Form people who know Scripture, grow in Christlike character, serve faithfully and help others follow Jesus.

Learning the way of Jesus together

Teach the Bible well

  • Read the full passage and its historical / literary context.
  • Ask: What does it say? What does it mean? How does it point to Christ? How should we respond?
  • Teach basic doctrine, biblical theology, interpretation, apologetics and discernment.
  • Use age-appropriate, accessible formats; check quotations, sources and translation.
  • Welcome honest questions and correct errors humbly.
🙏

Build sustainable practices

  • Scripture reading, prayer, worship and gratitude.
  • Fellowship, communion, generosity and serving.
  • Rest, Sabbath rhythms, care of the body and healthy boundaries.
  • Confession, forgiveness, reconciliation and wise accountability.
  • Mission, hospitality and sharing faith in everyday life.
👥

Small groups that grow people

A simple 60–90 minute pattern:

  1. Welcome and check-in.
  2. Short prayer and Scripture reading.
  3. Open questions and careful listening.
  4. One practical response for the week.
  5. Prayer requests shared only with consent.
  6. Invite, serve, follow up and train a co-leader.

Keep group boundaries, attendance/contact lists and children’s safeguarding clear.

A repeatable one-to-one mentoring conversation

Meet with purpose and care

  • Agree a time, place, length, confidentiality limits and purpose.
  • Begin with “How are you really doing?”—then listen without rushing to fix.
  • Read a short Bible passage in context and ask open questions.
  • Choose one practice or next step; make it realistic and voluntary.
  • Close in prayer only with consent; confirm the next meeting.

Keep the relationship healthy

  • Mentors are companions, not owners, saviours or secret-keepers.
  • Use open, accountable settings; follow the church’s boundaries policy.
  • Do not provide clinical counselling, legal advice or financial control outside your competence.
  • Refer risk, abuse, trauma, severe distress or complex needs to trained people / appropriate services.
  • Seek supervision; end or transfer a mentoring relationship transparently when needed.
Discipleship is relational and reproducible: every Christian is called to follow Jesus, and growing disciples learn to help others follow him. It is not simply a class, a title, a leadership pipeline or attendance at events.

Growing towards Christlikeness

What mature Christian life includes

Character before platform

Rooted conviction

Knows the gospel; loves Scripture; can explain core Christian beliefs; distinguishes the Bible’s teaching from cultural preference, personal opinion and untested claims.

Formed character

Grows in love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness and self-control; practises truth, purity, humility and integrity.

Healthy relationships

Honours dignity, listens well, seeks reconciliation, respects boundaries, resists racism and favouritism, and protects people at risk.

Discernment & Spirit-filled life

Prays and serves in dependence on the Holy Spirit; tests teaching and prophetic impressions by Scripture, Christlike character, community discernment and accountability.

Stewardship & vocation

Uses time, gifts, money, influence and work faithfully; serves without entitlement; lives generously without being manipulated.

Mission & multiplication

Shares faith graciously, welcomes newcomers, supports the poor and vulnerable, equips others, develops leaders and serves beyond the church.

For prophetic, healing and deliverance ministry: respect consent and dignity; never use a claimed revelation to control a person’s marriage, money, medical treatment or safety. Invite accountable discernment. Do not promise a healing outcome, discourage prescribed care, demand payment, or minister physically to someone without permission. Use trained pastoral oversight and referral pathways.

JCRGM formation pathway

Ten-session discipleship and maturity course

Teach · discuss · practise

Use after or alongside the foundations course. The aim is Christlike maturity and service, not merely completion of a workbook or qualification for a title.

Session 01 · Facilitator guide

Abide in Christ and live from your identity

Read: John 15:1–11; Colossians 2:6–7

Teaching aim: Root everyday obedience in union with Christ rather than performance or ministry status.

Teach the text

  • Read “abide” as continuing dependence expressed in trust and obedience.
  • Identity in Christ does not deny struggle; it gives a secure place from which to grow.
  • Fruit is cultivated over time through the Spirit and life with other believers.

Discuss together

  1. What does abiding look like in an ordinary work week?
  2. How can leaders distinguish spiritual growth from public visibility?

Practice this week: Choose one daily pause for Scripture and prayer.

Session 02 · Facilitator guide

Interpret Scripture faithfully

Read: Nehemiah 8:1–8; 2 Timothy 2:14–16

Teaching aim: Use a sound reading process in personal study and group discussion.

Teach the text

  • Observe repeated words, structure, audience and literary form.
  • Interpret a text in its biblical context before applying it to today.
  • Compare difficult interpretations with the wider teaching of Scripture and trusted leaders.

Discuss together

  1. Which common reading habit can pull a verse out of context?
  2. What question would you ask before applying this passage?

Practice this week: Lead a short study using observation, interpretation and application.

Session 03 · Facilitator guide

Prayer and sustainable spiritual practices

Read: Luke 11:1–13; Psalm 63

Teaching aim: Develop a life of prayer that includes praise, lament, intercession, listening and rest.

Teach the text

  • Spiritual practices are means of grace, not ways to earn God’s favour.
  • Fasting, prayer and study should be practised wisely, with health and pastoral considerations.
  • Rest and Sabbath rhythms protect faithfulness over time.

Discuss together

  1. How can prayer stay honest during grief or unanswered prayer?
  2. What practice needs to become more sustainable?

Practice this week: Set a realistic weekly rhythm that includes Scripture, prayer, worship and rest.

Session 04 · Facilitator guide

The Spirit, gifts and discernment

Read: 1 Corinthians 12:4–27; 1 Thessalonians 5:19–22

Teaching aim: Use spiritual gifts to build up the body and practise accountable discernment.

Teach the text

  • Different gifts serve one body; no gift makes a person superior.
  • Test teaching and spiritual impressions by Scripture, character, community and fruit.
  • A claimed revelation must never be used to control another person’s money, medical care or relationships.

Discuss together

  1. How should a church weigh a spiritual impression?
  2. What safeguards help a person say “no” to prayer or ministry?

Practice this week: Offer a gift in a supervised way and ask the recipient what was helpful.

Session 05 · Facilitator guide

Character, holiness and integrity

Read: Galatians 5:13–26; Titus 2:11–14

Teaching aim: Connect holiness with grace-shaped love and integrity across public and private life.

Teach the text

  • The fruit of the Spirit is formed in relationships and daily choices.
  • Repentance includes naming harm, making repair where safe and accepting accountability.
  • Public gifting does not excuse private misconduct.

Discuss together

  1. Which fruit of the Spirit is being tested in your current season?
  2. How does accountability differ from surveillance or shame?

Practice this week: Invite one trusted person to give specific, kind feedback.

Session 06 · Facilitator guide

Relationships, forgiveness and conflict

Read: Matthew 18:15–20; Romans 12:9–21

Teaching aim: Practise truth, direct conversation, fair process and safety-aware reconciliation.

Teach the text

  • Speak directly where safe; do not build factions through gossip.
  • Use impartial mediation for appropriate disputes, but never force mediation in abuse cases.
  • Forgiveness is not the same as access, trust or removal of consequences.

Discuss together

  1. What makes a conflict unsafe for private mediation?
  2. How can a church hear a complaint without retaliation?

Practice this week: Use a structured conversation to address a low-risk misunderstanding.

Session 07 · Facilitator guide

Stewardship, vocation and generosity

Read: Luke 12:13–34; 1 Peter 4:10–11

Teaching aim: Steward time, money, work, relationships and gifts as service to God and neighbour.

Teach the text

  • Generosity is willing, transparent and never purchased by a promise from a leader.
  • Work and family responsibilities are part of Christian vocation.
  • Church resources require documentation and shared oversight.

Discuss together

  1. How can personal giving remain voluntary and accountable?
  2. What resource could your group steward better?

Practice this week: Choose one practical act of service or generosity without publicising the giver.

Session 08 · Facilitator guide

Suffering, resilience and hope

Read: Romans 8:18–39; 2 Corinthians 1:3–7

Teaching aim: Offer presence and Christian hope without blaming people or promising quick relief.

Teach the text

  • Scripture makes room for lament and does not treat suffering as proof of weak faith.
  • Christian hope is anchored in God’s presence and resurrection, not guaranteed outcomes.
  • Leaders should refer trauma, illness and mental-health needs to qualified care.

Discuss together

  1. What phrases can unintentionally shame a grieving person?
  2. How can a group offer practical support while protecting privacy?

Practice this week: Offer one concrete, consent-based act of care.

Session 09 · Facilitator guide

Service, calling and responsible leadership

Read: Mark 10:35–45; Ephesians 4:11–16

Teaching aim: Serve within gifts and boundaries, and prepare others to share responsibility.

Teach the text

  • Christian authority is service accountable to the body.
  • A role needs a clear purpose, training, supervision and safe limits.
  • Mature leaders equip others rather than hoard information or platform.

Discuss together

  1. What would healthy delegation look like in JCRGM?
  2. What support does a volunteer need before taking a role?

Practice this week: Write a one-sentence role purpose and one growth goal.

Session 10 · Facilitator guide

Witness, multiplication and sending

Read: 2 Timothy 2:1–7; Acts 13:1–3

Teaching aim: Help others follow Jesus and strengthen the church by raising trustworthy disciple-makers.

Teach the text

  • Multiplication is passing on faithful teaching and example, not cloning personality.
  • Sending requires prayer, preparation, local accountability and practical support.
  • Review fruit and mistakes honestly; keep the gospel central.

Discuss together

  1. Who can you encourage without creating dependency?
  2. What should be in place before a new group or mission effort begins?

Practice this week: Mentor one person for a season and agree clear expectations and review dates.
