Teaching in church, Bible classes & groups

Teaching & Groups

A facilitator-ready system for biblical teaching that moves from text to understanding, practice and multiplication.

For pastors, teachers and group facilitators

Prepare and lead a Bible lesson that forms people

Text first · people always

Use the same disciplined teaching process in a Sunday service, JCRGM Bible School class, ministry-worker training or small group. The format can change; faithful handling of Scripture, pastoral care and a clear response should remain.

Before the session: prepare the text

  1. Pray, read the whole book section and identify the passage’s main idea.
  2. Note context, genre, repeated words, structure and original audience.
  3. Check the interpretation against Scripture as a whole and JCRGM’s approved doctrine.
  4. Write one sentence: “Because this text teaches ___, we should ___.”
  5. Choose 2–4 outcomes, one illustration that clarifies the text, and a voluntary practice.
  6. Prepare questions that invite observation and honest response, not the answer you want to hear.

During the session: teach for understanding

  1. Welcome and state the learning aim; make access and participation clear.
  2. Read the passage aloud; let learners observe before the teacher explains.
  3. Teach the main idea with context, explanation, illustration and application.
  4. Invite questions; acknowledge uncertainty and correct errors without shaming.
  5. Discuss response and pray only with consent; clarify referral or safeguarding limits.
  6. Close with one practice and a way to review it next time.

JCRGM church & group curriculum

12-session series: foundations of a strong church

Teach · discuss · practise

Use one series across Sunday teaching, Bible classes, ministry-worker formation and small groups. Each session has a passage, clear aim, teaching outline, discussion and weekly practice.

Session 01 · Facilitator guide

The gospel and the lordship of Christ

Read: Mark 1:14–20; Colossians 1:15–20

Teaching aim: Help learners explain who Jesus is and why the gospel shapes the whole church.

Teach the text

  • Read the call of Jesus in its setting.
  • Name the gospel announcement and the response Jesus calls for.
  • Connect church priorities to the lordship of Christ.

Full teaching guide · 15–20 minutes

Open and set context: Read Mark 1:14–20 and Colossians 1:15–20. Begin with Jesus’ announcement of God’s kingdom and the call to follow him. Ask learners to observe how the passage places Jesus—not church growth technique—at the centre.

Teach the main truth: Colossians presents Christ as supreme and sufficient; the church’s identity and mission flow from him. The gospel includes God’s rescue, Jesus’ death and resurrection, and a call to trust and follow. A church can run programmes and still lose its centre if success becomes attendance, prestige or a leader’s platform. Teaching should distinguish the heart of the gospel from secondary questions while remaining faithful to JCRGM’s doctrine. Let the passage shape what the congregation celebrates and what it changes.

Apply and pray: Ask what one ministry decision would look like if Christ’s lordship were the first test. Invite a three-sentence gospel summary grounded in Scripture. Pray that Jesus remains central in every gathering.

Discuss together

  1. What does Jesus announce?
  2. What would change if Christ shaped this decision?

Practice this week: Name one JCRGM practice that can more clearly reflect Christ’s lordship.

Session 02 · Facilitator guide

How to read a passage faithfully

Read: 2 Timothy 3:14–17; Luke 24:25–32

Teaching aim: Give learners a method to read Scripture in context.

Teach the text

  • Observe what the text says before asking what it means.
  • Consider context, genre, audience and structure.
  • Apply the main point in a way consistent with the whole Bible.

Full teaching guide · 15–20 minutes

Open and set context: Read 2 Timothy 3:14–17 and Luke 24:25–32. Explain that Scripture is opened in context and that understanding involves both careful reading and a response to God. Demonstrate the method on one paragraph.

Teach the main truth: First observe: repeated words, people, actions and structure. Then interpret: identify genre, setting, audience and the author’s main point, reading the surrounding verses. Finally apply: ask how this truth shapes belief, relationships and action today. Do not make a text say what a preferred illustration suggests. Compare interpretations with the wider Bible and ask trusted teachers about difficult passages. Application is strongest when it follows the actual point of the text rather than a verse taken alone.

Apply and pray: Work through one passage together, asking each person for an observation before offering an interpretation. Have learners write one application and one question. Pray for teachers who handle Scripture carefully.

Discuss together

  1. What did you observe that you missed on a quick reading?
  2. What application follows from the text?

Practice this week: Use the observation–interpretation–application method on a short passage.

Session 03 · Facilitator guide

Grace, repentance and assurance

Read: Ephesians 2:1–10; Romans 8:1–17

Teaching aim: Teach salvation as grace that brings assurance and transformed life.

Teach the text

  • Explain sin, the cross, resurrection, repentance and faith.
  • Distinguish grace from earning and from permission to harm.
  • Ground assurance in God’s promise in Christ.

Full teaching guide · 15–20 minutes

Open and set context: Read Ephesians 2:1–10 and Romans 8:1–17. State the central truth: salvation is God’s gift in Christ and grace forms a new life. Let the passage explain both the depth of need and the generosity of God.

Teach the main truth: People are not saved by achievement, spiritual status or giving; they are made alive by grace and called to walk in good works prepared for them. Repentance and faith are real responses, but they do not purchase God’s love. Assurance should rest on God’s promise in Christ, not on a leader’s ability to read someone’s heart. Grace also does not give permission to harm: it begins a changed life shaped by the Spirit. Hold invitation and patience together.

Apply and pray: Ask learners to distinguish “I must earn acceptance” from “grace frees me to obey.” Offer a private next step for questions about salvation. Pray with gratitude for grace and new life.

Discuss together

  1. What does grace make possible?
  2. Where does assurance come from?

Practice this week: Write one sentence about grace from the passage.

Session 04 · Facilitator guide

Prayer, worship and spiritual life

Read: Matthew 6:5–13; Psalm 63

Teaching aim: Form a church that prays honestly and worships beyond performance.

Teach the text

  • Use Jesus’ prayer as a pattern.
  • Make room for lament, gratitude, confession and intercession.
  • Connect gathered worship with daily obedience and care.

Full teaching guide · 15–20 minutes

Open and set context: Read Matthew 6:5–13 and Psalm 63. Invite learners to hear prayer as Jesus teaches it: oriented toward God, honest about human need and shaped by trust. Make room for silence and lament.

Teach the main truth: Worship is response to God, not a performance for an audience. The Lord’s Prayer gives a pattern for praise, surrender, provision, forgiveness and protection; the Psalms teach a vocabulary for longing and grief. A gathered service can form disciples when Scripture, prayer, song, communion and care point people toward faithful living. Leaders should explain what will happen and make participation accessible. No one should be shamed for not speaking or singing aloud.

Apply and pray: Review a typical JCRGM service: where do people hear Scripture, participate, receive care and take a next step? Ask one practical improvement. Pray for worship that forms love throughout the week.

Discuss together

  1. Which part of prayer is often neglected?
  2. How can a service include people who do not speak publicly?

Practice this week: Practise a short prayer pattern during the week.

Session 05 · Facilitator guide

The Holy Spirit, gifts and character

Read: 1 Corinthians 12:4–27; Galatians 5:16–25

Teaching aim: Teach spiritual gifts with discernment and Christlike fruit.

Teach the text

  • Gifts build up the body; they do not prove superiority.
  • Test teaching and prophetic impressions by Scripture, fruit and accountable community.
  • Teach the fruit of the Spirit alongside public ministry.

Full teaching guide · 15–20 minutes

Open and set context: Read 1 Corinthians 12:4–27 and Galatians 5:16–25. Explain that Paul places many gifts within one body and pairs ministry with the fruit of the Spirit. Ask learners to notice both diversity and mutual dependence.

Teach the main truth: Gifts serve the common good; they are not badges of superior faith. The Spirit glorifies Christ and grows love, patience and self-control in the church. Teachers should encourage gifts while testing claims by Scripture, character and accountable community. A claimed revelation must never override another person’s safety, medical care or freedom. Disagreement about gifts can be handled without ridicule or coercion. Public gifting and private integrity belong together.

Apply and pray: Invite learners to name a supervised way to use a gift and a character quality that should accompany it. Pray for a church where gifts build up everyone.

Discuss together

  1. How do gifts serve the common good?
  2. What safeguards make ministry safe?

Practice this week: Offer one gift in a supervised, consent-based way.

Session 06 · Facilitator guide

Belonging, baptism and the Lord’s Supper

Read: Acts 2:37–47; 1 Corinthians 11:23–26

Teaching aim: Clarify the church’s shared life and JCRGM’s practices.

Teach the text

  • Explain belonging, mutual care and participation.
  • Teach JCRGM’s baptism and communion practice with clarity.
  • Invite questions without pressure or shame.

Full teaching guide · 15–20 minutes

Open and set context: Read Acts 2:37–47 and 1 Corinthians 11:23–26. Explain how faith in Christ joins people to a community that learns, shares and remembers Jesus together.

Teach the main truth: Baptism and the Lord’s Supper should be explained clearly, not treated as ceremonies whose meaning everyone already knows. Teach JCRGM’s actual doctrine and process, including preparation and pastoral guidance, while welcoming questions. Church belonging means mutual service and care as well as receiving ministry. Do not pressure a person into a public step before they understand or consent. Newcomers benefit from a simple “what to expect” explanation.

Apply and pray: Ask what a newcomer should know before baptism or communion and how JCRGM communicates it. Pray for congregations that welcome people patiently and faithfully.

Discuss together

  1. What does a newcomer need to understand before baptism?
  2. How does the church care for members?

Practice this week: Create or review a newcomer “what to expect” guide.

Session 07 · Facilitator guide

Relationships, speech and reconciliation

Read: Ephesians 4:25–32; Matthew 18:15–20

Teaching aim: Practise truth and reconciliation while recognising abuse and power imbalance.

Teach the text

  • Teach truthful speech, anger and forgiveness.
  • Explain safe, fair conflict steps.
  • Never force mediation in abuse or coercion cases.

Full teaching guide · 15–20 minutes

Open and set context: Read Ephesians 4:25–32 and Matthew 18:15–20. Show how truth, anger, forgiveness and constructive speech shape a community. Explain that reconciliation is pursued with wisdom, not by silencing a harmed person.

Teach the main truth: Christian speech should be truthful and intended to build up, not humiliate or recruit a faction. Ordinary disagreements may be addressed directly where safe, with impartial help when needed. Forgiveness does not erase consequences or instantly restore trust. When abuse or coercion is alleged, a private confrontation or forced mediation may be unsafe; use safeguarding and independent complaint procedures. Teachers should model the same fairness they ask of the congregation.

Apply and pray: Practise rephrasing an accusation as a clear, respectful concern. Ask when that conversation would not be safe. Pray for honest speech, wise boundaries and peace with justice.

Discuss together

  1. What is the difference between forgiveness and restored trust?
  2. When is a group conversation unsafe?

Practice this week: Practise one low-risk reconciliation step.

Session 08 · Facilitator guide

Witness and mission

Read: Matthew 28:18–20; Colossians 4:2–6

Teaching aim: Equip the church for everyday witness and compassionate outreach.

Teach the text

  • Explain the gospel clearly and listen well.
  • Connect proclamation with service and community partnership.
  • Build consent-based follow-up into outreach plans.

Full teaching guide · 15–20 minutes

Open and set context: Read Matthew 28:18–20 and Colossians 4:2–6. Explain that Christian witness combines the message about Jesus with wise, gracious speech and a community that lives what it proclaims.

Teach the main truth: Evangelism is not a debate to win or a count to report. Teach learners to explain the gospel clearly, listen before answering and accept “no” without punishment. Compassionate service should respond to real needs and must not be conditional on conversion. A congregation also needs a follow-up pathway with separate consent for records and contact. The church can invite people into worship and discipleship while respecting each person’s freedom and pace.

Apply and pray: Ask groups to plan one small act of witness and service with a named leader, safety plan and voluntary next steps. Pray for neighbours and for a faithful, gentle witness.

Discuss together

  1. How can JCRGM serve without making aid conditional?
  2. What outreach is community-led?

Practice this week: Draft an outreach plan with safety, consent and follow-up.

Session 09 · Facilitator guide

Servant leadership and church ethics

Read: Mark 10:35–45; 1 Peter 5:1–5

Teaching aim: Teach authority as humble, accountable service.

Teach the text

  • Compare worldly status with Jesus’ model.
  • Clarify leader accountability and boundaries.
  • Make complaint routes accessible and independent.

Full teaching guide · 15–20 minutes

Open and set context: Read Mark 10:35–45 and 1 Peter 5:1–5. Contrast status-seeking with Jesus’ model of service. Explain that Christian leadership is accountable stewardship, not ownership of people or their loyalty.

Teach the main truth: Leaders need character, training, supervision and clear boundaries in addition to gifting. A healthy church can question a decision without being called rebellious. Senior leaders need an independent complaint route, conflicts of interest should be disclosed and authority should be shared where possible. No one should use prayer, prophecy, discipline or access to the pulpit to obtain sex, money or silence. These safeguards help the church serve with trust rather than fear.

Apply and pray: Ask who holds JCRGM leaders accountable and where a member can raise a concern safely. Pray for servants who welcome correction and use authority to equip others.

Discuss together

  1. Who holds leaders accountable?
  2. How can a leader share power?

Practice this week: Review one ministry role’s authority and reporting line.

Session 10 · Facilitator guide

Safeguarding and pastoral care

Read: Psalm 82:3–4; Matthew 18:5–6

Teaching aim: Prepare every worker to prevent harm and respond safely.

Teach the text

  • Use policy, training and safe activity planning.
  • Respond to disclosures without blame, secrecy promises or informal investigations.
  • Know referral and legal-reporting pathways.

Full teaching guide · 15–20 minutes

Open and set context: Read Psalm 82:3–4 and Matthew 18:5–6. Emphasise the church’s responsibility to protect children and vulnerable people. Safeguarding begins before an activity and continues in how concerns are received.

Teach the main truth: Every worker needs to know JCRGM’s written procedures, focal person and alternative reporting route. Safer recruitment, supervision, transport plans, consent for images and boundaries for online contact all reduce risk. If someone discloses harm, listen, protect immediate safety and report through the approved pathway; do not interrogate or investigate privately. Follow current local requirements and seek competent professional guidance. Prayer and pastoral care can accompany action but must not delay it.

Apply and pray: Run a short fictional scenario drill and ask each volunteer to state the first safe step and reporting contact. Pray for protection, courage and dependable care.

Discuss together

  1. What must be done immediately after a disclosure?
  2. Where is JCRGM’s policy and contact route?

Practice this week: Complete a safeguarding scenario review.

Session 11 · Facilitator guide

Stewardship, money and governance

Read: 2 Corinthians 8:16–21; Luke 16:10–13

Teaching aim: Connect generosity to accountable handling of church resources.

Teach the text

  • Giving remains voluntary; reject transactional spiritual promises.
  • Use budgets, dual controls, separation of duties and reporting.
  • Protect donor and beneficiary privacy.

Full teaching guide · 15–20 minutes

Open and set context: Read 2 Corinthians 8:16–21 and Luke 16:10–13. Connect generosity with integrity: faithful handling of money is part of the church’s witness.

Teach the main truth: Giving should be voluntary and not exchanged for a promised miracle, blessing or leadership favour. Church funds need an approved purpose, budget, receipts, shared custody, reconciliation and timely reporting. Separate duties where practical and disclose conflicts of interest. Beneficiary and donor privacy should be protected. The congregation should know which body reviews accounts and how concerns are raised. Apply JCRGM’s current finance policy and local legal requirements rather than relying on a sermon as an accounting procedure.

Apply and pray: Ask learners to map one offering from collection to report and identify one control that protects both the money and the workers. Pray for contentment, transparency and wise stewardship.

Discuss together

  1. How are church funds separated from personal funds?
  2. What is one control to strengthen?

Practice this week: Map one finance flow and identify oversight.

Session 12 · Facilitator guide

Multiplication and mature disciples

Read: 2 Timothy 2:1–2; Ephesians 4:11–16

Teaching aim: End the series by sending learners to teach, serve and form others.

Teach the text

  • Maturity includes character, doctrine, care and mission.
  • Develop others without creating dependence on one leader.
  • Review learning and agree a next pathway.

Full teaching guide · 15–20 minutes

Open and set context: Read 2 Timothy 2:1–2 and Ephesians 4:11–16. Explain maturity as a combination of sound teaching, Christlike character, mutual care and mission—not simply a title or a long time in church.

Teach the main truth: Leaders equip the saints so ministry is shared across the body. Multiplication means entrusting Scripture and responsibility to reliable people, then supporting them as they grow; it is not cloning one personality or creating a following. A mature church prepares successors, reviews its teaching and makes room for feedback. Learners should leave with a pathway for continued growth and a clear person to ask for support. Keep Jesus and the good news central as new workers are formed.

Apply and pray: Invite each person to choose a 30-day growth goal and one person they can encourage or mentor appropriately. Pray for the next generation of faithful disciples and teachers.

Discuss together

  1. What have you learned to pass on?
  2. Who will support your next step?

Practice this week: Create a 30-day action plan and review it with a group leader.
