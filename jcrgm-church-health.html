Church Health & Stewardship

Strengthen JCRGM’s church systems without losing the spiritual life, relationships and mission those systems serve.

The church that stands

Core foundations of a healthy local church

Strong in truth and love

A strong church develops both spiritual life and trustworthy structures. These foundations support one another; none should be used to excuse neglect of another.

⛪

1. Gospel & doctrine

Clear statement of faith; Christ-centred proclamation; biblical teaching; sound interpretation; theological formation for teachers.

🕊

2. Worship & prayer

Word, prayer, praise, communion/baptism according to church practice, pastoral intercession and freedom from spiritual coercion.

❤️

3. Community & belonging

Hospitality, membership and pastoral connection, intergenerational life, care for newcomers, conflict resolution and inclusion.

🌱

4. Discipleship & formation

A visible pathway from seeker to new believer to mature disciple and servant; small groups; leader development; mentoring.

🛡

5. Safe pastoral care

Safeguarding policy, trained volunteers, complaint/reporting process, privacy, referrals, crisis response and care for survivors.

⚖

6. Accountable leadership

Shared governance; clear roles; policies; healthy oversight; conflict-of-interest rules; succession planning and pastoral review.

💰

7. Stewardship & transparency

Approved budgets, reliable records, dual controls, separation of church and personal funds, honest fundraising and timely reporting.

🤲

8. Compassion & justice

Practical care, dignity, fair access, anti-exploitation, support for vulnerable families and wise partnerships with local services.

🌍

9. Evangelism & mission

Everyday witness, local outreach, digital presence, cross-cultural mission, church planting, unity and long-term community service.

JCRGM systems to establish and review

Governance & people

  • Constitution, statement of faith, leadership roles and decision-making limits.
  • Membership and newcomer pathway; accurate contact preferences and records.
  • Volunteer applications, role descriptions, training, code and reviews.
  • Staff care, performance conversations, leave, grievance and succession processes.
  • Conflict-of-interest register and fair purchasing / hiring practices.

Finance & stewardship

  • Annual ministry budget approved by oversight; regular budget-to-actual reports.
  • Two-person counting and documentation; prompt deposits; independent reconciliations.
  • Different people approve, pay, record and reconcile transactions where possible.
  • Written gift, benevolence, reimbursement, procurement and asset policies.
  • Respect donor intentions; report project funds truthfully; never promise spiritual outcomes for gifts.

Services, facilities & communication

  • Service plans, accessible welcome, clear announcements and predictable follow-up.
  • Emergency contacts, first-aid plan, evacuation routes and incident reporting.
  • Safe venue checks, equipment inventory, maintenance and event risk assessments.
  • Approved media / photography consent, accessible communications and moderation rules.
  • Data backup, access control, retention and a response plan for lost devices or exposed records.

JCRGM church health formation

Eight-session church-health course

Teach · discuss · practise

A leadership and congregation series for building healthy systems alongside prayer, Scripture, discipleship and mission.

Session 01 · Facilitator guide

The church’s identity and mission

Read: 1 Peter 2:4–10; Ephesians 2:19–22

Teaching aim: Describe the church as a people called to worship, witness, holiness and mutual service.

Teach the text

  • Start with Christ and the gospel before strategies or attendance targets.
  • Name the community JCRGM serves and the gifts already present.
  • Let church policy, Scripture and pastoral leadership guide local decisions.

Full teaching guide · 15–20 minutes

Open and set context: Read both passages aloud. Begin with the image of a living house: the church is not first a building, programme or crowd, but a people being built around Christ. Ask learners to notice the identities and responsibilities named in 1 Peter 2:4–10 and Ephesians 2:19–22.

Teach the main truth: Christ is the cornerstone and the source of the church’s belonging. Worship, teaching, holiness, welcome, service and witness flow from that identity; none is a marketing substitute for the gospel. Numbers can help leaders plan, but attendance alone cannot tell whether people are growing in Christ, receiving safe care or serving their neighbours. JCRGM’s plans should therefore begin with Scripture, its approved doctrine and the actual needs and gifts of the community it serves.

Apply and pray: Invite the group to name one gift already present in JCRGM and one neighbour or community need the church should listen to. Close by asking God to make the congregation faithful, welcoming and useful in Christ’s mission.

Discuss together

  1. What does a healthy church look like beyond attendance?
  2. Which parts of JCRGM’s mission are strongest and where is support needed?

Practice this week: Agree on three measurable, people-centred priorities for the next quarter.

Session 02 · Facilitator guide

Governance, accountability and decision rights

Read: Acts 6:1–7; 1 Peter 5:1–5

Teaching aim: Build structures that clarify authority and protect against unaccountable power.

Teach the text

  • Use constitution, approved policies and clear role descriptions.
  • Separate oversight from day-to-day tasks where possible.
  • Provide conflict-of-interest disclosure, complaints and review processes.

Full teaching guide · 15–20 minutes

Open and set context: Read Acts 6:1–7 and 1 Peter 5:1–5 in context. Explain that both passages connect leadership with care: a practical need must not be ignored, and authority is given for service rather than personal advantage. Ask who is heard when a decision is made.

Teach the main truth: Healthy governance makes responsibility visible. The constitution and approved policies establish the boundaries; role descriptions explain who may decide, who must be consulted and who reviews the result. The separation of oversight from daily tasks reduces both error and the risk that one person will control money, records and complaints. Disclosure of conflicts of interest, fair complaint routes and scheduled review protect trust. These arrangements do not replace prayer or spiritual maturity—they help the church practise them honestly.

Apply and pray: Use a simple JCRGM decision as an example: identify the owner, approval, record and review date. Ask what happens if the decision affects the person approving it. Pray for leaders who welcome accountability.

Discuss together

  1. Who approves policy, budget and appointments in JCRGM?
  2. Where is independent accountability needed?

Practice this week: Map one decision pathway from proposal to review.

Session 03 · Facilitator guide

Welcome, membership and belonging

Read: Romans 15:7; Acts 2:41–47

Teaching aim: Create a warm, consent-based path from visitor to connected member.

Teach the text

  • Explain what happens at services and how to ask for support.
  • Ask before collecting contact details; separate record and contact permissions.
  • Offer a human connection, discipleship opportunity and way to ask questions.

Full teaching guide · 15–20 minutes

Open and set context: Invite the group to imagine arriving at a JCRGM service for the first time. Read Romans 15:7 and Acts 2:41–47, then ask what welcome looks like after the greeting—not only at the door, but in the weeks that follow.

Teach the main truth: Christian welcome receives people as Christ has received us, without demanding instant trust or a public story. Belonging grows through honest information, relationships, shared worship and a clear way to ask questions. Contact details are personal information: permission to keep a record is different from permission to make contact. Explain who will use details and why, then honour a person’s choice. A newcomer should meet a real, accountable church contact and be offered—not pressured into—a group or next step.

Apply and pray: Practise a warm introduction that explains what happens next and allows the visitor to decline. Ask the group how JCRGM can make its next welcome clearer, safer and more accessible; pray for newcomers by name only with permission.

Discuss together

  1. What barriers might a newcomer encounter?
  2. How can the church follow up without pressuring?

Practice this week: Walk through a first-visit experience and improve one step.

Session 04 · Facilitator guide

Worship, teaching and discipleship systems

Read: Colossians 3:12–17; 2 Timothy 3:14–17

Teaching aim: Align pulpit, Bible class and small groups around Scripture and practical formation.

Teach the text

  • Plan a balanced teaching calendar under JCRGM’s doctrine and leadership.
  • Link Sunday teaching to group discussion, home practice and next-week review.
  • Train facilitators; provide accessible material and child-safe settings.

Full teaching guide · 15–20 minutes

Open and set context: Read Colossians 3:12–17 and 2 Timothy 3:14–17. Ask learners what changes when the same Scripture theme is heard in a service, explored in a Bible class and practised during the week.

Teach the main truth: Scripture is not merely information for a sermon; it trains the whole community in faith and life. A balanced calendar helps JCRGM teach the breadth of Christian belief and character rather than repeating only familiar themes. Facilitators can connect Sunday teaching to group questions, home reading and a review of practice the following week. Accessibility matters: clear language, readable materials, interpretation where needed and safe settings help more people participate. Every teacher remains accountable to the Bible and JCRGM’s approved doctrine.

Apply and pray: Have the group map one upcoming theme from pulpit to class to home practice. Ask which people might be left out by the current format, then pray for teachers to handle Scripture faithfully and gently.

Discuss together

  1. How can Sunday teaching carry into weekday life?
  2. What training does a group leader need?

Practice this week: Plan one four-week theme across service and small groups.

Session 05 · Facilitator guide

People, teams and operations

Read: Ephesians 4:11–16; Exodus 18:13–23

Teaching aim: Equip volunteers with purpose, preparation, safe practice and succession.

Teach the text

  • Match roles to gifts and real need; do not equate status with calling.
  • Provide induction, role charter, rota, training, feedback and support.
  • Review event, facility, transport and digital risks before activities.

Full teaching guide · 15–20 minutes

Open and set context: Read Ephesians 4:11–16 alongside Exodus 18:13–23. Notice that healthy leadership equips others and shares a load that would otherwise exhaust one person. Ask learners to name the difference between a title and a useful role.

Teach the main truth: A ministry role should answer a real need and have a clear purpose, limits and supervisor. Matching responsibility to gifts and training honours both the volunteer and the people being served; it does not rank one ministry above another. Induction, rotas, feedback and rest make service sustainable. Before an event, leaders should consider the venue, transport, children, digital communication and emergency arrangements. Planning is a form of care because people should not have to improvise their safety.

Apply and pray: Choose one JCRGM volunteer role and write its purpose, training need and reporting line. Ask what support the worker needs to serve well, and pray for shared leadership and a healthy pace.

Discuss together

  1. Which roles are overloaded or unclear?
  2. What can be delegated with supervision?

Practice this week: Create a one-page charter for a JCRGM ministry team.

Session 06 · Facilitator guide

Finance and faithful stewardship

Read: 2 Corinthians 8:16–21; Luke 16:10–13

Teaching aim: Handle giving and resources with integrity and shared controls.

Teach the text

  • Use approved budgets and timely reports.
  • Use two-person counting, documentation and separate duties where practical.
  • Protect donors’ privacy and never promise spiritual outcomes for contributions.

Full teaching guide · 15–20 minutes

Open and set context: Read 2 Corinthians 8:16–21 and Luke 16:10–13. Highlight Paul’s concern that the handling of a gift be honourable before God and people. Invite the group to distinguish generosity from pressure.

Teach the main truth: Church money is entrusted for ministry, not owned by an individual leader. An approved budget, receipts, two-person counting, separate duties and timely reports create a traceable account of how gifts are used. Smaller congregations may need practical adaptations, but should still avoid leaving one person unchecked. Donor privacy matters; public recognition must never be used to shame people into giving. No contribution purchases a blessing, position or pastoral favour. Apply JCRGM’s actual finance policy and local requirements.

Apply and pray: Walk through a hypothetical offering from collection to report and identify the person responsible at each step. Ask where a second check belongs. Close by praying for contentment, generosity and integrity.

Discuss together

  1. How can JCRGM show accountability without exposing individual givers?
  2. Which financial process needs a second check?

Practice this week: Review one control with the approved finance/oversight team.

Session 07 · Facilitator guide

Pastoral care, safeguarding and inclusion

Read: Isaiah 1:16–17; James 2:1–9

Teaching aim: Make the church safe, welcoming and able to refer needs wisely.

Teach the text

  • Use safeguarding procedures and accessible complaint routes.
  • Build a referral network for health, welfare, counselling and urgent danger.
  • Protect dignity across age, ability, background and social status.

Full teaching guide · 15–20 minutes

Open and set context: Read Isaiah 1:16–17 and James 2:1–9. Ask the group whom a church can overlook, and how partiality appears in ordinary decisions about attention, space, language or help.

Teach the main truth: Biblical care protects dignity and takes injustice seriously. A welcoming church needs written safeguarding procedures, accessible complaint routes and leaders who know when to refer beyond their competence. Listen without promising absolute secrecy, blaming a person or making a quick diagnosis. Children, adults at risk, people with disabilities and those experiencing poverty may face barriers to being heard; build safe ways for them to raise a concern. Compassion includes clear limits, trained support and referral to qualified health, welfare or counselling services.

Apply and pray: Invite participants to identify one barrier to care at JCRGM and one safe way to reduce it. Do not ask anyone to disclose private experience in the group. Pray for wisdom, protection and impartial love.

Discuss together

  1. Who could be unintentionally excluded from church life?
  2. How is a safeguarding concern raised and handled?

Practice this week: Complete a welcome/access/safety walk-through of one gathering.

Session 08 · Facilitator guide

Review fruit and improve faithfully

Read: Philippians 1:3–11; Luke 14:28–30

Teaching aim: Use data and reflection to make wise decisions without reducing spiritual life to numbers.

Teach the text

  • Review spiritual formation, care, mission, finance, risk and team wellbeing together.
  • Use aggregate data; restrict personal information; learn from failures and complaints.
  • Assign next actions, owners, resources and review dates.

Full teaching guide · 15–20 minutes

Open and set context: Read Philippians 1:3–11 and Luke 14:28–30. Explain that wise review looks honestly at what a church is building while keeping spiritual maturity and neighbour-love in view.

Teach the main truth: A dashboard should serve discernment, not turn people into scores. Combine aggregate measures—participation, training, safeguarding readiness, financial reporting and community service—with careful reflection on Scripture, care, belonging and worker wellbeing. Attendance may be useful, but it is not proof of discipleship or health. Protect personal information; present only what leaders need to make a responsible decision. When something fails, learn without hiding it or blaming the person who raised a concern. Every review should end with an owner, resource, next action and date to check progress.

Apply and pray: Ask the group to choose three balanced indicators for the next quarter: one for formation, one for care and one for mission or stewardship. Pray for honest learning and the courage to improve.

Discuss together

  1. Which measures encourage health rather than competition?
  2. What did the last outreach or teaching series teach us?

Practice this week: Hold a 60-minute quarterly review and publish an appropriate summary.
