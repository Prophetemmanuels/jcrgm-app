Before you go

Prepare the team and the outreach

Approval · purpose · people · safety
Confirm authority and purpose.
Obtain the JCRGM leader’s approval; name the community need, invitation and outcome you hope to serve—not a target for pressured decisions.
Listen before planning.
Ask residents or trusted local partners what is useful, who is already serving and what time or location works for them.
Name the accountable lead.
Set a team leader, safeguarding focal person, check-in contact, meeting point, finish time and an agreed way to leave if anyone feels unsafe.
Review risks and permissions.
Check venue approval, weather, transport, first aid, accessibility, child-safe practice, photography, referral options and emergency arrangements.
Brief every volunteer.
Practise the gospel in plain language, consent phrases, listening, boundaries, refusal, disclosure response and the follow-up process.
Agree resources and money.
Approve the budget and who may spend it; record materials taken and returned; never promise help or aid that has not been authorised.

Suggested team roles

Team lead

Confirms approval, brief, safe boundaries, headcount and return check-in.

Conversation partners

Work in pairs, listen, ask permission and respect the person’s choice.

Safety focal person

Knows the JCRGM route for safeguarding concerns and can arrange a safe exit or referral.

Materials / follow-up steward

Distributes approved tools and handles only consented records under church policy.

Pack with purpose

Field materials checklist

Take only what the plan needs

Core outreach kit

  • Bibles or approved Bible-reading cards in the languages volunteers can use.
  • JCRGM gospel and service invitation flyers (downloadable below).
  • Printed listening questionnaires, clipboards and pens—offer, never insist.
  • JCRGM response cards, stored and handled only under the consent rules.
  • Approved contact / location information and next-step options.
  • Charged phone, agreed check-in contact, transport plan and water.
  • Approved accessibility aids, name badges or identification if required by the activity.

Before distributing a handout

  • Check the phone number, email, service times and map link are current.
  • Ask permission before leaving a flyer in a shop, school, workplace or private property.
  • Never place leaflets where littering, trespass or disruption may result.
  • Use the language and large-print version that people can read; explain any QR / link.
  • Do not photograph people, take testimonials or post stories without specific consent.
  • Take unused flyers and litter away; thank hosts and community partners.

A respectful gospel conversation

Six steps volunteers can practise

Human first · no pressure

1 · Greet and ask permission

Introduce yourself and the JCRGM team. Do not start with a speech or presume the person’s beliefs.

“Hello, we’re with Jesus Christ Redeems Global Ministries. Would you be open to a short conversation about faith, or would you prefer not today?”

2 · Listen with respect

Use open questions, allow silence and reflect back what you heard. Do not interrogate, debate or assume what a person needs.

“What has your experience with church or faith been like? Is there a question you’d like to explore?”

3 · Share the good news clearly

God created people with dignity; sin harms our relationship with God and one another; Jesus died and rose again; by grace we can trust him and begin a new life. Keep Jesus at the centre and invite questions.

4 · Offer prayer or a next step—by choice

Ask whether the person would like prayer, a Bible conversation, a service invitation or simply to finish the conversation. A no or “not now” is accepted warmly.

“Would you like to pray or hear more another time? It’s completely okay if not.”

5 · Ask separately about recording and contact

Permission to keep a limited record is not permission to contact. Use the questionnaire and response card only as explained; if consent is absent or unclear, do not keep identifiable details or make contact.

6 · Close with dignity

Thank the person. Do not make aid, friendship or welcome conditional on a decision. Give a flyer only if it is wanted and safe; debrief as a team without sharing private disclosures.

Protect people and volunteers

Safeguarding and practical safety

Follow approved JCRGM procedures

Team behaviour

  • Work in pairs or accountable teams; use visible, public settings and the agreed check-in plan.
  • Do not arrange private one-to-one contact with a minor. Follow guardian consent and JCRGM child-protection policy.
  • Do not take or publish images, personal stories, phone numbers or prayer requests without specific informed consent.
  • Do not promise absolute secrecy. Collect the minimum information and use only approved secure storage.
  • Respect personal space, culture, disability, religious difference and a person’s right to stop.

If someone discloses harm or immediate danger

  1. Listen calmly; thank them for telling you; do not blame, interrogate or ask leading questions.
  2. Check immediate safety and get help from the team lead / safeguarding focal person.
  3. Do not confront the alleged person or investigate the matter yourself.
  4. Record only necessary facts securely and report through JCRGM’s approved route and competent authorities as current policy / law requires.
  5. For urgent medical or safety danger, follow the local emergency procedure. Seek professional support; prayer does not replace urgent care.

Do not improvise legal advice. Confirm current Zambian reporting and data-protection requirements with JCRGM leadership and qualified local advisers.

Learn and improve

Team debrief after every outreach

Care · truth · next steps

Reflect together

  1. What did we learn by listening to the community?
  2. What questions or next steps did people freely request?
  3. Did everyone feel safe, respected and free to say no?
  4. What worked, what was difficult and what should change?
  5. Were any materials, funds or consented records handled outside policy?
  6. Who owns the next action and when will leadership review it?

Measure what matters

Record only approved, aggregate information: team hours, flyers offered, conversations welcomed, referrals provided and follow-up requested. Do not inflate decisions, store names without permission or treat a person as a number.

Faithful fruit: truthful witness, safe care, people heard, useful community service, disciples supported and workers prepared for the next step.
