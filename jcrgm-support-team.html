How it works

Four steps, from need to receipt

  1. 1
    Apply or be registeredA member applies on their phone, or the Support Team registers a beneficiary being sponsored.
  2. 2
    Vetting & referenceTwo team members verify the details — documents, a home visit or a phone call to the referee.
  3. 3
    Decision & paymentThe committee approves, declines or defers. Money is paid as far as possible straight to the school or clinic.
  4. 4
    Receipt & follow-upA proof reference is recorded, a review date is set, and the sponsor is told what became of their gift.

Before you apply

What we ask of you — and what we promise

⚠️
Apply honestly. Every case is checked. A false claim ends support immediately and is reported to church leadership. A genuine need, however small, is always welcome.
🔒
Your privacy. Names, phone numbers and NRC details are seen only by the Support Team and are held under the Data Protection Act No. 3 of 2021. We never publish a beneficiary's name or photograph without written consent.
💰
No cash is ever promised. Support is given as far as possible by paying the school, clinic or supplier directly, and everything is receipted.
🚨
In an emergency — a child at risk, violence at home, or a medical crisis — do not wait for this form. Call the office now, or go to the nearest police station or hospital.

    Good practice

    Standing orders of this desk

    1
    Two-person rule. No disbursement is made on one signature. The assigned member and one other must both appear in the file notes.
    2
    Pay the institution, not the pocket. School fees go to the bursar, treatment to the clinic. Record the receipt number the same day.
    3
    Close the loop. Every applicant is told the outcome in Decision letter, and every sponsor gets a one-paragraph report on their gift.
    4
    Children and the vulnerable. Where a minor is involved, a guardian's consent must be ticked, and no photograph leaves this device without it.

    Where the data lives. A case is saved to this church's own Supabase project over HTTPS — the same project the Announcements and membership pages use — and from there it appears on every phone that runs the app. Reading the whole register needs a signed-in, leader-approved account in a Support Team role and the team code; the database enforces the same rule, so a modified copy of this page cannot get around it.
    What the public form can do. It can file a case, and look up one case with the reference number plus the last four digits of the phone that applied. It cannot list, search or browse anyone else's file — that is a database policy, not a setting.
    If the phone has no signal. The entry is kept in this phone's own browser storage and uploaded later. Anyone holding this phone and opening this page can read that queue, so do not leave the app open on an unattended device, and do not store what does not need to be stored. A phone code is a deterrent, not a vault: the register is protected by the church account and the database rules, and by keeping the desk itself out of strangers' hands.
    What not to type here. This is a welfare register, not a filing cabinet. No HIV or clinic letters, no national ID images, no court or family-dispute detail, nothing a child should never read back. If a case needs that, it belongs in the pastoral office on paper, referenced here by one line saying it exists.
